Fiery Void has a huge variety of factions, so this document gives a short overview of each one - a feel for what it is and how it plays.

The entries were written by different authors: some introduce a faction's playstyle and systems, while others simply point out the important things to know when playing it in Fiery Void.

The Tier Ratings group the factions into competitive tiers, to help players pick fleets that will be reasonably well matched in a pick-up game. There is a degree of judgement in this, but with so many factions, an idea of each one's relative strength is more helpful than not!

Custom designs

Some factions (and units) are marked custom: they are not official designs released by AoG, and may not be as well balanced as the official factions. Before using custom units it is good practice to check that your opponent is OK with them - or, if you are creating the game, to say so in its description.

Semi-custom units

Some units are marked semi-custom: not official AoG designs, but not considered fully custom either, for a variety of reasons. For example:

  • official units that could not be fully ported into Fiery Void for technical reasons, but were balanced with some changes (the Brakiri Tashkat, for example);
  • units that expand a faction's fleet-building options while keeping close to official designs (the Ipsha Jumpsphere, for example);
  • units released by AoG after the company had officially stopped producing B5 Wars material (Showdowns-10 and Variants-6, for example).

Once known as the Lion of the Galaxy, the Centauri Republic's glory days may be over - but it can still field a powerful fleet, potent both at range and up close.

Its ships tend to be very specialised, and often quite fragile, so players need to play around this to secure victory.

Plasma Stream

A plasma weapon that permanently reduces the armour of the systems it hits. It also counts full armour for every rake - unlike normal raking weapons.

Guardian Array

  • Primarily a defensive weapon, able to intercept fire aimed at a third party if it lies between the firing unit and its target.
  • In the tabletop game it has to be shut down to switch between offensive (anti-fighter) and defensive modes. Fiery Void has no such requirement: Guardian Arrays can intercept or fire offensively at will.
  • The definition of "between" has changed too: in Fiery Void the weapon is between the firing unit and its target if the bearings to them differ by at least 120 degrees (2 hex sides).

Chameleon Sensors

  • An ELINT array (with all the usual ELINT abilities) that can also disguise its ship as a different vessel. Carried by the Dargan Strike Cruiser.
  • When you buy the ship, the enhancement options include a free "Chameleon Suite" choice: pick any other Centauri hull, or leave it on "None". Your enemies then see that ship - its silhouette, name, damage sheet, weapons and defensive ratings all belong to the vessel you picked, not to the Dargan. Your own team always sees the truth.
  • Shots at a disguised ship resolve against both sheets from a single die roll: your opponent sees their shot land on the ship they think they are firing at, while the real damage goes on the real hull. The disguise gives no defensive benefit - a Dargan hiding inside a smaller hull is exactly as easy to hit as a Dargan.
  • Weapon arming status is always hidden from your enemies, disguised or not, and stays hidden even after the deception is broken: they can never tell which of your guns are loaded.
  • The suite can be switched on and off in Initial Orders, alongside the power controls. Switching it off is permanent - the disguise does not come back.

Seeing through a Chameleon disguise

Detection is tracked per team, exactly as for stealth ships: in a multi-team game, a team that has seen through the deception faces the real ship, while everyone else still faces the simulacrum. A disguise is broken by:

Proximity
An enemy ship within 5 hexes, with line of sight, sees through it immediately (2 hexes for fighters and shuttles).
Implausible manoeuvring
Changing speed faster than the ship you are imitating could.
Implausible ELINT
Running an ELINT operation the imitated hull has no array for, or spending more EW than its sensors could produce.
Firing the wrong weapon
A shot only looks convincing if the imitated ship mounts a weapon of the same class whose arc covers that shot, and each gun you fire must be matched by a different one on the simulacrum. Fire more Matter Cannons than it carries and the extra one gives you away. This reveal takes effect at the start of the following turn - your opponent works out the discrepancy after the fact.
Losing the array
If the Chameleon sensors are destroyed, or switched off, the deception ends at once and for good.
Ancient sensors
Ancient and Primordial races (Shadows, Vorlons, Kirishiac, Mindriders, Thirdspace) are never fooled at all. If any team fields such a unit, Chameleon ships are revealed to that team from the start of the game.

Once revealed, the ship stays revealed to that team for the rest of the game - switching the suite back on will not restore the illusion, for example.

The Centauri fleet of a few hundred years before the show era, from the Wars of the Centauri Republic (WotCR) period.

Much weaker than the modern Centauri, as you would expect, but intended to compete against other fleets of that period.

Sentinel Point Defense

A precursor of the Guardian Array (see Centauri Republic) that works the same way defensively, but has no offensive mode.

A now-extinct race that once terrorised the League of Non-Aligned Worlds during the Dilgar War.

The Dilgar use a mix of ballistic, particle and plasma weapons to great effect, dealing large amounts of damage at any range.

Pentacan Formation

Dilgar ships can benefit from the Pentacan formation, whose rules are simplified in Fiery Void. Dilgar ships gain an initiative bonus while within 10 hexes of a command ship (not cumulative). A fighter flight has +5 Initiative as long as its flight leader (the first craft in the flight) is alive and uninjured - that is, has taken no damage. Dilgar fighters also have an inherent -2 dropout bonus.

Point Pulsar

A dedicated weapon for called shots: it suffers only half the usual penalty for making one (-20% instead of -40%).

Mass Driver

Fitted to many Dilgar ships. It works in Fiery Void, but it is a planetary bombardment weapon of mass destruction, of limited use in space combat (although it can engage starbases and immobile Enormous ships). It is a Matter weapon that always hits structure (never systems), and it can be intercepted without degradation, like a ballistic weapon. The firing ship must be at speed 0 to use it.

Although relatively new on the galactic scene, the Earth Alliance has quickly become a major power, playing a pivotal role in the Dilgar War.

Since its near-extermination at the hands of the Minbari, Earthforce has come back bigger and stronger: its modern ships are notable for their firepower and survivability, at the cost of manoeuvrability and limited firing arcs.

Interceptor

A dedicated defensive weapon that can be boosted in Initial Orders to let it target enemy fighters (it cannot intercept while boosted). It generates an Energy Web (E-Web): a shield-like effect over its arc that reduces the hit chance of all incoming fire by its intercept rating. Fighters cannot fly under an E-Web, and it does NOT reduce damage.

Aegis Sensor Pod

A specialised system found only on the Hyperion Aegis Cruiser. It targets a fighter flight and hits automatically, giving the firing ship 3 CCEW against that unit. This bonus EW cannot be combined with any other OEW, CCEW or EW from another Aegis Pod. The Pods stand out on the ship's hull, so called shots against them get a +10% bonus (-30% instead of the usual -40%).

The early Earth Alliance units are semi-official custom designs, all deployed before 2200. They generally lack the better defences of later Earth designs, and many use blast cannons.

Some official units, such as the Olympus Alpha, appear in both the standard Earth Alliance list and the early one, to represent their transition from high-end units of the early EA to very old units of the Dilgar War era.

The famous Starfuries (Tiger, Nova, Aurora) are not available, but the early members of the Starfury line - the Flying Fox, Aries and Atlas - laid the foundation of the fighter-heavy EA fleets of the future.

The oldest of the Younger Races, and the most technologically advanced - as their powerful Jammer and high-damage weaponry show.

Minbari fleets have few apparent weaknesses at first, but the cost of their ships means they will usually be outnumbered, which can be a distinct disadvantage.

Jammer

A powerful defensive technology that stops enemies getting a lock-on with their Electronic Warfare, so range penalties against Jammer-protected units are always doubled. It also halves the launch range of ballistic weapons. The Minbari themselves ignore Jammers, as do races with Advanced Scanners (the Ancients, for example). On Minbari fighters, Jammer protection does not stack with jinking.

Gravitic Drives

Ships can manoeuvre even while pivoted or pivoting, using the thrusters that suit their current orientation. Gravitic Thrusters also ignore the first "Efficiency Reduced" critical they receive, which makes them more resistant to damage.

Antimatter Converter

A weapon whose damage depends on how well it hits - the hit chance minus the to-hit roll. It uses Flash mode, so it deals 25% damage to other units in the same hex as any target it hits.

Gravitic Net

Used to move a target; it fires in the Pre-Firing phase, before regular weapon declarations. First target a ship (friend or foe): a green hexagonal marker shows the hexes the target can be moved to. Then target one of those hexes to confirm the shot.

Electro-Pulse Gun

A very short-ranged, slow-firing weapon that only affects fighters - but it can make called shots with no penalty.

White Stars

  • The mainstay of the Army of Light is available in the Minbari fleet list. Difficult to handle, but extremely potent in skilled hands, it carries Vorlon technologies (see Vorlon Empire) nominally beyond the Younger Races' reach - Adaptive Armor, EM Shields and, on the Command variant, even self-repair.
  • White Stars can also be fielded as an ISA / Army of Light / White Star Fleet. Such a fleet is NOT tournament legal - and for good reason - but it may make an interesting scenario piece (or challenge). It is far too strong for regular pickup battles without asking your opponent first! Used this way, only White Stars (and any Combat Flyers they can carry) may be deployed. Be warned: in skilled hands such a fleet is simply overwhelming.

A former Centauri colony, the Narn Regime is now a galactic power in its own right. What its vessels lack in technology, they make up for with powerful weaponry and sheer survivability.

Energy Mines

  • A hex-targeted weapon that deals heavy damage to everything in the hex it hits, and splash damage to everything in the adjacent hexes - allies included. Devastating against fighters, and when used en masse (6+ Energy Mine launchers can easily saturate an area).
  • Opponents cannot see where you targeted your Energy Mines until firing is resolved - only that they were launched. Launched mines also have a 25% chance to scatter or dissipate harmlessly.

Pulsar Mines

An automated short-range weapon that fires at passing fighters. It tracks enemy fighters automatically during the Movement Phase, and attacks any that came within its arc and range before the Firing Phase begins. Each Pulsar Mine can fire up to 18 shots a turn.

The Centauri's arch-nemesis in the Wars of the Centauri Republic, Orieni fleets are known for the amount of matter weaponry they bring, their wide range of medium ships, and their Hunter-Killer drones.

A peculiar faction in many ways: oppressive to play against because of their damage potential, yet difficult to win with if you are not experienced with them.

Hunter-Killer (HK) Drones

  • The Orieni long-range weapon of choice, and probably their most outstanding feature: essentially large missiles with a degree of control, which use fighter rules and attack by ramming. HKs must start the game deployed in hangars.
  • Hunter-Killers are controlled by Control Nodes on Orieni ships, each able to control one flight of HKs per point of output shown on the system. Without enough control, HKs operate autonomously but suffer the Uncontrolled critical effect: they head for the closest enemy ship and try to ram it automatically, jink for two levels automatically, and suffer -15 Initiative.
  • When ramming, Hunter-Killers suffer a hit-chance penalty based on their own speed (the faster they move, the worse it is). Because ramming attacks happen before all other fire, HKs that reach ramming distance cannot be shot down before they try to ram.

Light Gatling Railgun

The Orieni Templar's gun is Matter-based, which makes it a threat to even the biggest ships. The downside is that it runs out of ammunition after 6 shots; more can be bought as an enhancement in Fleet Selection.

Strike Force

  • The Orieni military is split into branches, and a few ships have different limitations when deployed in a Strike Force detachment rather than the regular navy, the Hand of the Blessed.
  • Assume any force led by a Paragon is a Strike Force, while lesser command ships lead the Hand of the Blessed. The Fleet Checker does not take this rule into account.

Not a single faction with a standard force list, but the various pirates, privateers and wanderers of the Babylon 5 universe.

Specific raider groups are named in the unit name (for example 'Centauri Privateer'); any unit without a faction prefix is a generic raider that any group can field. A Fleet Checker for every possible combination is next to impossible, so choosing an appropriate fleet takes a bit of player responsibility. The raider mini-factions in Fiery Void are summarised below.

Brakiri Shokhan

  • The Shokhan grew out of Brakiri groups that never integrated into modern Brakiri society. Many operate purely as raiders, but some are suspected of receiving outside support from Brakiri corporations or others, and act as privateers and sometimes mercenaries.
  • They have three Brakiri-specific ships, plus a limited selection of generic raider units: the Aspar, Galleas, Hawk, Ma'Ri'e, Felucca and Xebec. Their two fighter designs are the Delta-V and the Folshot-A.

Centauri Privateers

  • Generally led by lesser nobles of the great houses, or by minor houses looking to advance, Centauri privateers have existed throughout the Centauri's time in space. They first operated against other Houses, but turned more to the edges of Centauri space as other powers were discovered.
  • As well as Centauri hulls, they use a limited selection of generic raider hulls: the Ma'Ri'e, Ma'Ri'u, Pinnace, Uid'Ac'e, Felucca and Xebec. They use Delta-Vs almost exclusively, with restricted access to Razik fighters or older Centauri fighters such as the Glaive and Phalen.

Drazi Hunters

  • Born of the Drazi love of hunting, and typically drawn from Drazi disaffected with traditional Drazi society. The Hunters are more a raider group than privateers, and are not officially endorsed by the Drazi - but their members are welcome in Drazi space to a limited degree.
  • They operate three units of Drazi design and a few generic raider hulls: the Aspar (limited), Galleon, Hawk, Ma'Ri'e, Pinnace and Wolf Raider (limited). Their only fighters are Cobras and Delta-Vs.

Independent Mercenaries League (IML)

  • Formed during the Dilgar War, originally by out-of-work Belt Alliance forces. Various League powers have supported it since, and it typically has better access to technology than traditional raider groups.
  • The IML fields three unique units - the Attack Cruiser, Armed Transport and Missile Frigate - along with their variants.
  • It uses Armed Shuttles and Delta-Vs almost exclusively, with restricted access to Star Snakes and Lellat fighters.

Imperial Star Legion

  • Operates mainly within Centauri, Earth and Narn space.
  • The Legion has three unique hulls - the Starjammer, Gladius and Augustus - and is known for heavy weapons and fusion cannons.
  • It has operated every generic raider unit at one time or another, and uses Delta-V and Double-V fighters exclusively.

Junkyard Dogs (JYD)

  • A raider group that supported the Army of Light in the Shadow War, but had bigger plans for the future and ended up fighting the Minbari Protectorate.
  • They operate several converted hulls from the main powers: a Lias, two Tethys, a Kutai, a Mograth, two Vorchans, two Sho'Kos and a Thentus.
  • They mainly use Armed Shuttles, Delta-Vs and Double-Vs, but may also field a flight of Raziks or Goriths.

Narn Privateers

  • Formed from Narn elements thought too radical or unruly to fit into the newly free Narn Regime, these privateers were sent out to keep striking at the Centauri. The more organised groups received more support from the Regime, and carried out missions ranging from traditional raiding to intelligence gathering.
  • They operate three Narn-specific units and a selection of generic raider designs: the Brigantine (limited), Hawk, Ma'Ri'u, Pinnace and Xebec. They mainly use Delta-V and Double-V fighters, plus a restricted number of Narn Goriths.

Tirrith Free State (TFS)

  • Born from an EA base built in the Tirrith system during the Dilgar War. The raiders who took the base over eventually formed an independent star system where several powers meet; the TFS is now less a raider group than a tiny nation.
  • The TFS has three unique hulls - the Blockade Runner, System Monitor and Freedom Base - and will use any generic raider unit.
  • Most TFS fighters are Delta-Vs, but it can also field Armed Shuttles, Double-Vs, Goriths, Star Snakes and Koists. Its only defence satellites are Drazi Dudromas.

Torata Privateers (Custom)

  • Based on units created by Jason Stadnyk on The Great Machine. These Torata forces explored Markab and Vree space in search of resources and technology.
  • The Uala's low population makes Torata Privateers relatively uncommon, with very few units.
  • They operate only two Torata-specific units, plus a limited selection of generic raider designs: the Hybrid Saucer, Caravel, Freebooter, Galleon and Aspar.
  • They mainly use Armed Shuttles, Delta-Vs and stripped-down Tukas, and may field a normal Tuka as a rare variant.

The League of Non-Aligned Worlds is a group of 14 playable factions, released in the Babylon 5 Wars books Militaries of the League 1 and 2.

They have a huge variety of special abilities and playstyles, and although their power varies dramatically from one faction to another, they can all be a lot of fun to play.

Gravitic Shield

  • Most Abbai units carry Gravitic Shields. An active shield reduces both the hit chance and the damage of incoming fire on its arc. At range 0, fighters are assumed to fly underneath the shields and effectively ignore them.
  • Gravitic Shields are powered by a shield generator, which can only power a limited number of them - usually not enough for all-round protection, unless its output is boosted.

Quad Array

  • Four guns in one housing, prone to overheating. Firing more than 2 shots offensively gives one or two 'May Overheat' critical effects the following turn (for 3 or 4 shots fired).
  • While that critical is in place, firing the Quad Array offensively again causes a critical roll at the end of the turn, at +2 for every shot fired that turn, and -2 if there is only one May Overheat critical.

Comms Disruptor

Reduces the target's EW and Initiative for the next turn.

Particle Impeder

  • A purely defensive weapon, able to intercept weapons with the "uninterceptable" trait.
  • It can also be boosted with EW rather than the usual power. A boost raises the Impeder's intercept rating, and also counts as a special kind of shielding that reduces the hit chance of all incoming weapons - all fighter fire included.

Shield Projector

  • Only seen on the Abbai base and OSATs. It can target allies within 10 hexes, and boost their Gravitic Shields that turn by the Projector's rating.
  • It effectively fires as a ballistic weapon, with its effect applied in the Firing Phase. To get the shield bonus, the allied unit must be within 5 hexes of the projecting unit at the end of the Movement Phase.

The Abbai fleet of a few hundred years before the show era, from the Wars of the Centauri Republic period.

One of the weaker factions overall, but it has Gravitic Shields much like the modern Abbai (though much weaker), and weapons that are precursors of the modern Comms Disruptor (the Comm Jammer and Sensor Spike).

Corporations

  • The Brakiri are split into competing corporations. Each unit notes which corporation produces it; a unit with no note is assumed to be available to all corporations equally.
  • This does not matter for a combined Brakiri fleet, but you can field a single corporation for flavour. You then have only the designs that corporation can use, but all restrictions (deployment and variant) are eased by one level: for variants Rare → Uncommon → Common, for deployment Restricted (10%) → Limited (33%) → Unlimited.
  • Each corporation has slightly different technology and its own specialities, so its fleet is usually lacking in some areas but excellent in another. The Fleet Checker does not take this rule into account.

Gravitic Shield

  • Most Brakiri units carry Gravitic Shields. An active shield reduces both the hit chance and the damage of incoming fire on its arc. At range 0, fighters are assumed to fly underneath the shields and effectively ignore them.
  • Gravitic Shields are powered by a shield generator, which can only power a limited number of them - usually not enough for all-round protection, unless its output is boosted.

Gravitic Drives

Ships can manoeuvre even while pivoted or pivoting, using the thrusters that suit their current orientation. Gravitic Thrusters also ignore the first "Efficiency Reduced" critical they receive, which makes them more resistant to damage.

Gravitic Bolt / Gravitic Pulsar

These can be boosted with power to increase their damage and intercept rating, which gives them a cooldown period equal to the boost level used. Fired at maximum boost, the weapon may also suffer critical damage.

Gravitic Shifter

Fires in the Pre-Firing phase. Target an ally or an enemy to try to change its facing by 60 degrees, clockwise or anticlockwise (using the matching firing mode). If it hits, the target is rotated before that turn's Firing Phase - so Gravitic Shifters can be used to escape enemy firing arcs, or to bring enemies into your ships' arcs. Only ONE Gravitic Shifter can be used on a ship per turn; any further Shifter attempts on it automatically miss.

Gravitic Mine

  • A ballistic weapon that targets a hex rather than a ship. The target hex is hidden from the opponent, and only revealed in the Pre-Firing phase, when the mine detonates. The mine cannot be intercepted and causes no collateral damage.
  • Any unit within 5 hexes of an active mine is pulled one hex towards the closest mine. Where two or more mines overlap and catch a target between their fields, the unit is instead 'sheared' - taking damage scaled by its size class and its closeness to the nearest mine, ignoring armour, with the side hit set by the furthest mine in range. Terrain, fixed Enormous bases and other mines are unaffected.

Poor Acceleration

For ships, this means very high acceleration costs. For fighters, the normal acceleration cost is doubled, but the medium and lighter fighters make up for it somewhat with unusually high thrust ratings: they cannot accelerate very well, but are exceptionally manoeuvrable even at high speed.

Ultralight Fighters

The smallest Cascor fighters are Ultralight, taking up only half a slot of hangar space meant for larger fighters.

Ioniser

The Cascor fighter weapon. Unlike typical fighter weapons it cannot be intercepted - and it cannot intercept either.

Ion Field Generator

An area-effect weapon, targeted on a hex rather than a ship. Units caught in the area have their capabilities reduced next turn: their Initiative, Power, EW and Offensive Bonus, plus a chance of a system shutting down.

Points Re-Evaluation Enhancement

  • The consensus in Fiery Void was that the Cascor were a fun faction but too expensive ever to be competitive. To correct this, an enhancement was added to their units that does nothing but change their price.
  • This has made the faction much more playable, and better balanced against other Tier 2 opponents. A custom variant of their attack fighter was made available too.
  • This is a player initiative, and using these enhancements turns the fleet into a Custom faction - so check with your opponent!

A very solid, beginner-friendly faction that pummels its enemies with an assortment of fast-recharging particle weapons.

Their distinctive HCV layout - outer hulls port and starboard instead of the usual front and aft - makes for surprisingly resilient ships.

Repeater Gunsights

Ships with Particle Repeaters can buy this in Fleet Selection for each Particle Repeater separately, as a System Enhancement from the ship window (see Available Refits). A repeater with gunsights can split its shots between different enemy units within a 1-hex radius of its original target, and between different fighters in the same flight. If the Particle Repeater takes any damage at all, it loses its gunsights. All the other Particle Repeater rules stay the same.

The Drazi fleet of a few hundred years before the show era, from the Wars of the Centauri Republic period. No notable rules or technologies.

Despite using hulls repurposed from other factions, Gaim ships are actually very good, and their variety is excellent - making the Gaim one of the stronger League factions.

Bulkheads

  • A defensive system that can absorb damage in place of another system, greatly increasing the resilience of Gaim systems. Bulkheads protect the systems on the structure they are mounted on; primary systems are not protected.
  • Whether to use a bulkhead is decided automatically: it aims to prevent a system's destruction, or kicks in when the structure block's integrity falls too low.

Particle Concentrator

  • Several Concentrators can combine into a single, more accurate and more powerful shot. Every combining ship must be within 1 hex of every other, and all of them must be hitting the same side of the target.
  • To combine, allocate the Concentrators in the combined firing mode you want, at the same target (the game picks the eligible partners automatically). If not enough eligible Concentrators are allocated, the shot resolves in the highest mode actually achievable.
  • The hit chance is averaged across the combining weapons, but normalised to the range of the combining ship closest to the target. If any combining ship has no lock-on against the target, the range penalty is doubled.
  • Damage is rolled once for the combined shot, and increases by +1d10 per additional weapon, up to a maximum of 5 additional weapons (+5d10).

Packet Torpedo

A ballistic weapon with a range penalty, like a direct-fire weapon - although only after its first 10 hexes of travel. Its target is not known to your opponent until impact.

Scattergun

A light weapon that fires a random number of shots. When firing defensively its shots go at separate threats - never at the same shot more than once.

Connection Struts

An advanced race of the League of Non-Aligned Worlds, whose ships all use a distinctive 'saucer' design.

The Vree use antimatter weapons almost exclusively, which deal more damage the further the to-hit roll beats the base hit chance. So their weapons are powerful at close and medium range, but typically less effective at longer distances.

Vree Ship Layouts

  • Vree capital ships have six outer hull sections, like starbases. Each has its own structure and works largely like the sections of a normal four-sided capital ship - but destroying an outer hull structure does not destroy the systems and weapons on that section.
  • Incoming fire rolls to hit a weapon, a thruster or structure, and is then allocated at random to a suitable system in the firing ship's arc.
  • Different weapons from the same ship can hit different hull sections of a Vree ship, which helps it keep some of its durability in battle. A single shot cannot be split between sections, though, which leaves the Vree vulnerable to raking weapons (and, to a lesser degree, pulse weapons).

Antimatter Weapons

  • Calculate range penalties in a non-standard way: for the first few hexes there is no penalty, then the normal penalty, and at longer ranges a double penalty. With no lock-on, the range itself is doubled (rather than the range penalty), and their range-reduced critical does not affect the range penalty itself - instead it adds 3 hexes to the effective distance to the target.
  • They calculate damage in a non-standard way too. Rather than being random, it is based on how far the to-hit roll beat the number needed (called X): every 5 points of difference is 1 point of X, and the damage formula uses X in place of the usual dice roll. The damage-reduced critical on an antimatter weapon reduces X by 2 (but not below 0).

Orbitals

The most unusual Kirishiac system: small segments of the ship that float over the hull, mounting various weapons and systems. There are three types.

All orbitals
An orbital is either docked or deployed, with no penalty to the ship or the orbital for either. Orbitals can start the match docked or deployed; after that, click the system icon in the Firing Phase to switch, and the change takes effect at the very end of the turn (after firing is resolved). If the associated ship structure block is destroyed, all of its orbitals are lost.
Docked
The orbital's structure merges with its structure block. Its weapons can be deactivated for power, and self-repair can be used (heavy orbitals have a special rule, below).
Deployed
Weapons may be used freely. The ship's self-repair cannot be used on the orbital, and its weapons cannot be deactivated for power. Overkill on a damaged weapon goes to the orbital's structure; if that is destroyed, any further overkill is lost. All orbitals can be targeted directly - select the orbital as if making a called shot (with no called-shot penalty). This uses any offensive EW on the parent ship, but the orbital has a smaller profile.
Light orbitals
Smaller, and cannot regenerate on their own. If destroyed they dock automatically, and the ship's self-repair must rebuild the orbital's structure and weapon before it can be used again.
Medium orbitals
The most common. They can also regenerate: if an orbital's structure is destroyed it docks automatically, and after 5 turns the orbital and its systems regenerate fully - its weapon can then be used for power or deployed again, as long as the associated ship structure block has not been lost.
Heavy orbitals
Much larger heavy weapon platforms, which CANNOT regenerate. The systems the Mindriders use, with a short description of each and any notable differences from their tabletop versions.

Special Hull Arrangements

Mindrider ships do not follow the usual layout: as a rule, the arcs of a ship's Thought Shields (see below) show you the arcs of its structure. The arrangement lets Mindrider vessels pivot for free, and spares them the usual hit-chance penalties for pivoting and rolling. The Wheel of Thought is unusual in that it MUST pivot every turn.

Constrained EW

Every Mindrider ship can use ELINT abilities, but on a 'constrained' basis: each ability costs 1 more EW than on a normal ELINT vessel.

Gravitic Drives

Ships can manoeuvre even while pivoted or pivoting, using the thrusters that suit their current orientation. Gravitic Thrusters also ignore the first "Efficiency Reduced" critical they receive, which makes them more resistant to damage.

Mindrider ships and fighters are protected by Thought Shields. Each shield covers a specific arc, and absorbs enemy fire on that arc up to its maximum hit points. The shields regenerate fully at the start of each turn, and hit points can be moved between arcs in Initial Orders to reinforce particular arcs.

Contraction

The Mind's Eye can Contract itself in the Movement Phase, gaining +1 to its Thought Shields and -5 to its Defence Profiles per level of Contraction; every 3 levels also give ALL its systems +1 Armour. To use it, click the + and - buttons by its special icon, then apply thrust as usual. Contraction lasts until the end of the turn, then resets to 0.

The systems the Shadows use, with a short description of each and any notable differences from their tabletop versions.

Ship Layouts

Even capital-sized Shadow ships use the MCV layout, and systems on Shadow ships cannot be targeted by called shots.

Energy Diffusers

  • Absorb damage as it is dealt (and diffuse it over time). In Fiery Void the diffuser always kicks in if it can - there is no option to hold it back for more threatening damage and save its capacity.
  • For visual reasons, the tendrils are shown as separate, untargetable systems, and damage they absorb is not shown in the log.
  • Diffusers do not try to stop no-overkill damage that would destroy the system hit despite them - which matters for very strong Matter or Piercing shots. Flash damage is treated specially: the part of the damage enough to destroy a system is treated as a whole shot, so systems can be saved even when the shot's total damage is very high.

Bio-Drive

  • Shadow ships have omnidirectional thrusters that generate their own thrust. For technical reasons, Fiery Void represents them as a series of untargetable thrusters of unlimited rating, plus an untargetable engine rated at the sum of all the biothrusters - so any thruster can provide thrust in any direction.
  • Biothrusters have their own criticals (which reduce output) in place of regular thruster criticals. The Bio-Drive cannot be boosted.

Half Phasing

  • During the Movement Phase, Shadow ships can Half-Phase, becoming semi-immaterial until the end of the turn.
  • A half-phased ship is much harder to hit (-20% to hit from normal weapons fire, -40% from ballistics), but also less accurate itself (-50% to hit with all shots). It cannot ram or be rammed at all, unless both ships are half-phasing, and it does not collide with terrain it moves through, as long as it does not end its movement in a terrain hex.
  • Half-Phasing costs the full thrust of two undamaged BioThrusters, and needs an online, undamaged Phasing Drive. Any damage to the Phasing Drive while half-phased destroys the ship.

Pilot

Shadow ships have no crew, but they do have a pilot, shown in the ship window as the C&C. The main differences from a regular C&C:

  • The pilot cannot suffer criticals, but feels pain when the ship is damaged (temporary) or when it is wounded.
  • Pilots cannot be repaired by a self-repair system. Telepathic attacks on Shadow ships are not implemented in Fiery Void.

Molecular Slicer Beam

  • Uninterceptable and armour-ignoring, with high fire control and good range, it has a very high damage output and can literally cut ships apart when fully charged. It has a number of other traits too, some of them simplified for Fiery Void.
  • The most powerful version, the Heavy Slicer - found only on Primordial ships - fires in Piercing mode by default, and can even overkill at full power so its damage potential is not wasted. Its Raking mode is capped at 2 turns' charge.
  • All other Slicers default to Sweeping mode, which lets them target as many different enemies as they have dice or damage to share out, assigning d10 target dice and/or set damage to each shot, up to their maximum. Simply select the weapon and choose your targets in this mode - each ship can only be targeted once, but fighter flights can be targeted several times. Splitting shots costs a cumulative -5% for every shot after the first, as well as any modifier for defensive shots (see below).
  • A Slicer may commit 1d10 dice, or 6 points of set damage, to gain -10 intercept, using the green 'Self-Intercept' shield icon and the menu it opens. Each self-intercept die committed increases both the number of different shots the Slicer can intercept and its total interception. If you fire offensively, or commit self-intercept dice, but do not use all of your dice or damage, the unspent dice are added to interception - as long as you committed at least ONE self-intercept die. You can track defensive fire in the ship tooltip. Each self-intercept die counts as a 'shot' for the -5% penalty above. The Light Slicer has no intercept rating, and cannot intercept at all.
  • A Slicer can also be pointed at one specific incoming shot, rather than leaving the game to place its defensive fire. In the Firing phase, select the Slicer, click the unit being shot at to bring up its tooltip, and click the hit chance of the shot you want to engage in its INCOMING list (manual interception is described in full in the FAQ).
  • A shot named this way costs exactly what a self-intercept costs - one damage die, or one whole 6-point block of set damage - out of the same pool, and counts as a 'shot' for the -5% penalty in the same way. Capacity you have already bought is re-used rather than paid for twice: if you hold self-intercept dice that are not yet pointed at anything, naming a shot spends one of those instead of charging the pool again. Withdrawing works from the ship window as usual, and hands the die or damage block back.
  • In a target's tooltip, each Slicer shot in its INCOMING list is written with the allocation you gave it - for example 1x Molecular Slicer (Sweeping) (3d + 12) for three damage dice plus twelve points of set damage.
  • The Slicer can make called shots against fighters with no penalty.

Phasing Pulse Cannon

Pulse weapons that completely ignore any kind of shield or shield-like defence used by the Younger Races. EM shields, like those on the White Star, count as Ancient even on Younger Race ships.

Vortex Disruptor

  • A weapon that destabilises hyperspace vortices, preventing enemy escape. It is fired at hexes where vortices are formed or forming. If it hits, any ship passing through the vortex that turn is destroyed automatically - unless it is an Ancient. Ancients may survive the collapse, depending on their distance from the vortex and how well the Vortex Disruptor rolled to hit: they roll 1d100 against the shot's to-hit margin plus 5 × the distance the ship travelled that turn, escaping on an equal or higher roll.
  • The game resolves the shot (working out the hit chance and reporting the result) and shows the result in the Combat Log. The Vortex Disruptor cannot be fired while a Shadow ship is half-phased.

Primordial Variants

Ships with the Primordial and Additional Tendril enhancements built in as standard, since neither option is available in Fiery Void.

Primordial Battle Cruiser

This ship differs from modern, quick-grown Shadow vessels in a few ways:

  • Most importantly, its main weapon's arcs: the Heavy Slicer on the Shadow Battlecruiser has both forward and rear arcs, and you can split its sweeping fire between them.
  • Six separate Energy Diffusers would have been hard to set up clearly on PRIMARY, so in Fiery Void they have been moved to the appropriate sides, with the hit charts adjusted.

Destroyer

Its original design has an acceleration cost of 1.5, which is not possible in Fiery Void, so it has been set to 2 instead.

Shadow Fighters & Fighter Bombs

  • Shadow fighters are semi-autonomous slivers made from their carriers' hull material.
  • Some Shadow carriers grow their fighters internally and hold them within the hull rather than parking them in space. These integrated fighters are bought as an enhancement on the carrier, up to its listed hangar capacity.
  • Such a carrier does not launch its fighters in the usual way. It deploys them with a Fighter Bomb - a forward-arc weapon, fired in the Firing Phase at a target hex within range. The held fighters appear at that hex the following turn, with the carrier's heading and speed. Fighters cannot be launched (or recovered) while the Shadow ship is half-phased.
  • You choose how many fighters to commit per shot, up to the number held. Launch more than a single flight can hold, and the bomb forms several flights at the target hex - split for you by default (largest flights first), but you can set each flight's size by hand instead, and add extra flights so a large launch is broken into smaller groups.
  • Unlike an ordinary launch, firing the Fighter Bomb carries no initiative penalty - neither on the fighters nor on the carrier. (Recovering fighters still costs the usual hangar-operations penalty.)
  • Integrated fighters return by landing on the carrier as normal, where they are reabsorbed into the hull: the ship repairs their damage, but that damage - plus any energy held in their diffusers - is drawn back into one of the carrier's own Energy Diffusers (with any excess penetrating to a random system), and they can launch again from the following turn. An empty hangar - whether the fighters were spent or lost - leaves the Fighter Bomb nothing to launch.
  • Shadow fighters have a massive -100 dropout bonus, so they pass all normal dropout tests easily - but they can still be dropped out by weapons that cause dropouts without a test. Most of those are Electromagnetic, and ignored by Advanced Armor anyway, but technically some Ancient weapons that cause automatic dropouts might still affect a Shadow fighter.
  • To make the most of their resilience - Energy Diffuser protection plus dropout immunity - Shadow fighters use their own hit allocation, which tends to spread damage across the flight more than usual.
  • Shadow fighters carry an accelerator weapon, so it is not used for interception unless explicitly ordered to!

As well as boosting systems with power, the Thirdspace faction can channel its psychic energy (represented by EW points) into its weapons, self-repair systems and Psychic Field.

The systems the Vorlons use, with a short description of each and any notable differences from their tabletop versions.

Capital Ship Layout

  • In B5 Wars, the Lightning Cannons of Vorlon capital ships are usually tied to both the forward and the relevant side sections, so they do not fall off too easily. In Fiery Void they sit on virtual "side-front" sections that cannot be hit directly and have no structure of their own.
  • The 'side-aft' sections are, to all intents and purposes, the ship's actual sides, and behave as such. Lightning Cannons appear on both the Front and Side hit charts, as in the tabletop game - but Front has separate entries for the port and starboard guns.
  • Weapons on these pseudo-sections can still be engaged by called shots from their own arc.

EM Shield

Works exactly like a Gravitic Shield, but needs no separate generator: each shield reduces both the hit chance and the damage of incoming fire on its arc by its rating. At range 0, fighters are assumed to fly underneath the shields and effectively ignore them.

Adaptive Armor

  • Extra protection that effectively raises all of a unit's armour ratings against specific types of damage.
  • Pre-assigned AA points can be set at the start of the battle, on Turn 1 only (if the ship's design allows it - the White Star is not advanced enough), or in the Initial Orders of later turns after taking damage from a weapon type.
  • Protection is limited both in the total number of AA points available, and in the most that can go on any one kind of damage.
  • The AA system's propagate button shares AA settings across your whole fleet. Assigned points cannot be unassigned during the battle.
  • For fighters, each fighter can have different AA settings, and AA points are unlocked for the whole flight (except in Super Heavy Fighter flights).

Power Capacitor

Produces the power of Vorlon ships, and can hold power between turns, up to its maximum. Key points:

  • New power is produced in Initial Orders, and is shown and usable from the start of that phase: the Reactor display already includes this turn's recharge (up to the Capacitor's maximum), so power drawn at the end of the previous turn - by weapons fire, or by a Jump Drive - is topped back up before you allocate anything. Power production can be doubled (along with Self Repair at the end of the turn) at the cost of deactivating all weapons and shields that turn.
  • Separately, you can open the petals on some Vorlon ships: this reduces the armour of all the ship's primary systems by 2 and increases its Defence Profiles by 5% that turn, but adds 50% to power generation.
  • Destroying the Capacitor leaves the ship powerless, but causes no catastrophic explosion like a Reactor's. In Fiery Void it leaves the ship powerless (the Capacitor being the main power source on Vorlon ships), and adds a Power reduction critical to the Reactor (so the ship goes out of control) and to the Self Repair system (so the damage is not simply repaired in a few turns).
  • Vorlon fighters also have petals, which they can open in Initial Orders for 2 extra thrust that turn - at the cost of +5% Defence Profiles and -2 side armour.

Mag-Gravitic Reactor

  • For technical reasons, Vorlons in Fiery Void use a Mag-Gravitic Reactor, so destroying power-using systems frees up their power. Note that the Jump Drive is the only Vorlon system with a power requirement at all - everything else on the hull is nominally 0.
  • The Scanner uses no power: its running costs are a feature of the base hull, and do not affect power use. The Jump Drive is the exception - the one Vorlon system with a real power requirement, though it only draws it on a turn it is actually used (see Jump Drive below).
  • All Vorlon weapons nominally use 0 power. They still need power to actually fire, but that is only decided, and taken from the Reactor, at the moment of firing.

Jump Drive

  • Vorlons open ordinary jump points rather than jumping out directly as the other Ancients do (see Ancient Jump Drives under About the Ancients) - and they can throw one a very long way: up to 12 hexes from the ship, against the standard 4. Being an ordinary jump point, it can be shot out by a Vortex Disruptor: the Vorlons and The System are the only Ancient-era fleets still vulnerable to one.
  • Unlike every other Vorlon system, the Jump Drive does use power, drawn from the Power Capacitor: 8 on the Planet Killer; 6 on the Strike Cruiser, Heavy Cruiser and Heavy Carrier; and 5 on the Battle Destroyer, Destroyer Escort, Dreadnought, Heavy Destroyer, Light Carrier, Light Cruiser and Scout. The Transport has no drive at all.
  • It is a per-use cost, not a standing one. The drive draws its power only on a turn it is actually used - the turn it opens a jump point, and every turn it maintains one. On any other turn it costs the ship nothing, so a Vorlon with an idle jump drive has its full power available.
  • When it is used, the cost is reserved out of your power allocation in Initial Orders, and drawn from stored power at the end of that turn. Opening and holding cost the same, so a jump point opened on one turn and held for three more costs the drive's power requirement four times. The Capacitor's recharge tops the store back up at the start of the next Initial Orders, so a ship whose recharge covers the cost starts every turn full.
  • Once you set Maintain Vortex, it carries over automatically to each following turn until you turn it OFF - you do not have to declare it again every turn. If the Capacitor cannot cover it, the Initial Orders power check says so before you commit.
  • In exchange, a Vorlon is exempt from the all-systems-dark rule: where any other race must shut down every powered system for the whole turn to hold a jump point open, a Vorlon simply pays the upkeep and fights on as normal.
  • And a paid-for jump point has no four-turn limit: it stands as long as the upkeep is paid. The system icon counts the turns open without a denominator ("5" rather than "5/4"), and the Maintain toggle stays available indefinitely.
  • If the Capacitor cannot cover the upkeep at the end of the turn - drained by weapons fire, emptied or halved by a critical, or shot out altogether - the jump point closes, and the combat log says so. Everything else still applies as normal: the jump point also closes if its holder is destroyed, drifts more than 12 hexes away, or simply stops declaring Maintain.
  • Arriving as reinforcements works the same way. Opening a jump point exit from hyperspace uses the drive, so it costs the power requirement on the turn the exit forms (reserved in that turn's Initial Orders, as for any other use). If the Capacitor cannot pay, the exit collapses at the end of that turn before anything comes through it, and the wave waits in hyperspace with nothing spent. Once the ship is on the board it may maintain its exit like any jump point - paying the upkeep, with no four-turn limit - and bring a fresh wave through on every turn it does.

Gravitic Drives

Ships can manoeuvre even while pivoted or pivoting, using the thrusters that suit their current orientation. Gravitic Thrusters also ignore the first "Efficiency Reduced" critical they receive, which makes them more resistant to damage.

Lightning Cannon

  • It has the accelerator property only so that it needs explicit permission to intercept (and draw power). This does not affect the weapon in any other way - leaving it to recharge for two turns does not make its shot more powerful, for example.
  • Lightning Cannons can combine for more powerful shots: give the right number of cannons the same firing mode and target. If the player mis-declares, with too few weapons for the declared mode, each of those shots fires as 1 Prong instead, at the same target, drawing 1 power, and the combat log notes it. The commit window warns you about any such shot before you commit your fire orders.

Lightning Gun

  • Each Lightning Gun is a single system that fires up to twice per turn. (Strike Cruisers in games started before this change still show the older version: a Lightning Gun plus a separate "Mirror Lightning Gun".)
  • Each click on a target declares one shot in the gun's current firing mode. The mode can be changed between clicks, so the two shots may go at different targets, in different modes. Each declared shot, and its mode, is listed in the target's incoming fire list.
  • A single gun fires in 1 Prong (Light) mode. Heavier modes combine one shot from each of several different Lightning Guns on the same ship (a gun cannot combine its own two shots together):
    • 2 Prongs (Medium, Raking) - 2 guns.
    • 3 Prongs (Heavy, Raking 15) or P3Piercing (Heavy, Piercing) - 3 guns.
    • 4 Prongs (Mega, Raking 20) or Q4Piercing (Mega, Piercing) - 4 guns.
  • To fire a combined shot: select the Lightning Guns you want to combine, set them all to the same firing mode, and click the target. Each selected gun declares one shot, and they fuse into a single shot when fire is resolved. Click the target again to fire a second combined shot with the same guns. All the shots being combined must have the same target, the same firing mode and the same called system (if any).
  • If a combined shot is declared without enough Lightning Guns, each of its shots fires as 1 Prong instead, at the same target, drawing 1 power, and the combat log notes it. The commit window warns you about any such shot before you commit your fire orders. A combined shot that does fire appears in the log as a single shot.
  • Power is drawn from the Power Capacitor for each shot separately: 1 for 1 Prong, 3 for 2 Prongs, 9 for 3 Prongs and 18 for 4 Prongs. Each gun shows its own share of the power for the shots it takes part in.
  • All modes are uninterceptable. As with the Lightning Cannon, the accelerator property only means the gun must be explicitly ordered to intercept; each unused shot may then intercept, drawing 1 power per interception.

Discharge Gun & Discharge Cannon

  • Can split up to four shots between several targets, and between offensive and defensive fire. Manually selected intercept shots use minimal power, and only if needed - that is, if they actually try to intercept an incoming shot.
  • Higher power levels are firing modes, so choose one before targeting any enemy ship. Every offensive shot fires at the same power level.

Planet-Cracker Beam

  • The signature weapon of the Vorlon Planet Killer: an Ancient superweapon meant for very specific scenarios rather than normal balanced play. Instead of being aimed at a target, it is activated in the Firing Phase and, once fired, automatically sweeps the four hexes directly ahead of the ship along its current facing.
  • Anything in those hexes - friend or foe alike; the beam does not discriminate - is automatically destroyed. That includes every hex of a multi-hex Terrain/Huge unit caught in the sweep, and every fighter of a flight caught in it, not just one.
  • It cannot be intercepted, ignores jinking and line of sight, and always hits.
  • Its recharge time is extremely long, so in practice it can only be used once per battle.

Vorlon Primordial Fighters

Super Heavy Fighters that use regular hangars, one Primordial fighter counting as two heavy fighters for fleet design and hangar purposes. In Fiery Void they are treated as a rare variant of Heavy Fighters, except in Primordial times, when Assault Fighters should be treated as common.

Skin Enhancements

Vorlon units may take only one Skin Colouring enhancement (Fiery Void does not enforce this - it is up to the player to comply). In summary - the full details are under Faction Enhancements in Ammo, Options & Enhancements:

Amethyst Skin Colouring
Improves Adaptive Armor, for players wanting more flexibility.
Azure Skin Colouring
EM Shield improvements, for greater overall protection.
Crimson Skin Colouring
A Power Capacitor improvement, for greater… power.

The Walkers are the enigmatic inhabitants of Sigma-957: an Ancient race that surveys and maps the galaxy rather than conquering it.

Below are the systems the Walkers use, with a short description of each and any notable differences from their tabletop versions.

Electromagnetic Weaponry

  • Every Walker weapon is of the Electromagnetic weapon class, and counts as Ancient (First One) technology for any rule that asks.
  • Some of them are accelerators: they gain damage when fully charged, and they do not begin the battle fully charged. A freshly deployed Medium Lightning Array or Chromatic Pulse Driver starts turn 1 with one turn of charge (1/2) rather than two. The one exception is The Wanderer, whose weapons do start the battle fully charged.

Lightning Array / Medium Lightning Array

  • The Array fires discharges. Each click on a target declares one discharge; a further click on the same target fuses another discharge into the shot already there, rather than declaring a second one. So four clicks on one ship produce one four-discharge bolt, while four clicks on four ships produce four single ones.
  • Fusing several discharges changes the weapon's damage, fire control and range penalty. A fused shot is not simply a bigger one: it is markedly better against capital ships, and markedly worse against fighters.
  • Two firing modes, both usable in the same turn:
    • Combined Fire (default) - repeat clicks on one target fuse into a single shot.
    • Single Shots - every click is a separate one-discharge order: what you want against a fighter flight, where four small shots are usually better than one larger shot.
  • 'Remove a firing order' peels one discharge off a combined shot rather than deleting the whole thing, and the shot's hit chance is re-priced at its new size. A single-discharge order simply goes.
  • The Medium Array is also an accelerator: its pool is one discharge per turn charged, up to two. That is why it cannot combine its shots until it has charged for at least two turns - with one turn of charge there is only one discharge, and nothing to combine.
  • Both weapons have an intercept rating and can be used defensively, and both spend available discharges when they do.

Wide-Beam Lightning Array Enhancement

  • A per-mount refit available to Ancient hulls only, bought in the lobby as a System Enhancement by clicking the Lightning Array system: 300 points for a full Lightning Array, 200 points for a Medium one, one level each. Two Arrays on the same hull are refitted separately.
  • The purchase buys the capability; the wide beam itself is a per-turn toggle in the Array's activation box, declared in the Firing phase, and it applies in whichever firing mode is used.
  • A wide beam trades power for spread: -2 on every damage die, to a minimum of 1 per die. Flat damage bonuses are untouched.
  • It doubles the weapon's collateral damage to 50% of the damage scored - and it is the one flash weapon that still scores collateral damage inside an Energy Draining Field, where it falls back to the ordinary 25%.
  • Firing a wide beam costs the Array its next turn: the weapon does not recharge on the turn advance after a wide-beam shot.

Chromatic Pulse Driver

  • An accelerator pulse weapon with two firing modes:
    • Pulse - charged one turn: D3 pulses, up to 4, at 14 damage. Charged two turns: D5 pulses, up to 8, at 18 damage.
    • Scanning - a single shot that does no damage at all, but analyses the target's shielding when it hits.
  • A scanning hit on any unit carrying a shield-type system banks one point of shield adaptation against that race, and the adaptation is fleet-wide: from the next turn on, every Walker in the fleet treats that race's shields as one point weaker, in both hit chance and damage reduction. Further scans stack.
  • 'That race' is the target's faction exactly as written, so (for example) the Brakiri and the Abbai are adapted to separately, even though both use Gravitic Shields.
  • Overlapping shields are not cumulative, and neither is the adaptation: it is applied once, against the strongest shield source protecting the target - including the shield types that hold a capacity pool rather than a flat rating.
  • Adaptation is public knowledge, so a defender can see how many times their shields have been scanned.

Energy Draining Field

  • A permanent, omnidirectional field centred on the ship, covering a radius in hexes (5 on a Traveler's field, for example). It can be deactivated like any other system, and a Variable field can be run at double power, in the Ship Power segment of Initial Orders, for a larger radius.
  • The field is public - it is drawn on the map for everybody, because an energy drain is a visible phenomenon. Overlapping field hexes count once, and the rest of the fleet that deployed the field is immune to it.
  • Shooting through a field is more difficult. Every field hex a shot crosses (your own fleet's fields excepted) is a -1 penalty on the d20 to-hit table. For Plasma and Antimatter weapons belonging to a younger race the penalty is doubled, and Plasma also treats the crossed hexes as extra range for its damage falloff.
  • Flash and proximity weapons lose their collateral damage and their blast radius inside a field, friend and foe alike. The only exception is a Wide-Beam Lightning Array.
  • The drain. Any unit that ends its turn in somebody else's field has the following drained for the next turn, resolved at the Critical Hit step:
    • 1d10 thrust - Engine output, or free thrust on a fighter flight;
    • 1d10 power - Reactor output; a reactor drained to zero blacks out every powered system on the ship;
    • 1d10 initiative - ×5 in FV units, and the total penalty cannot exceed -100;
    • 1d6 total EW.
  • Every further consecutive turn ended in a field adds one more die to the total already in force, so the drain only ever grows while a unit stays put. Nothing but initiative can be driven below zero. Enormous units are limited to the first die, and never escalate.
  • A fighter flight in a field must also roll for dropout on 2d10 instead of the usual 1d10, plus a further 1d10 per successive turn, and a grounded flight cannot shoot the following turn.
  • Additional fields do not stack: standing in three overlapping fields drains exactly as standing in one.
  • Criticals shrink the field: a fixed field loses a hex of radius per critical result (to a minimum of 1), while a variable one loses its double-power bonus first, then a hex at a time (to a minimum of 0).

Extended Draining Field Enhancement

  • Buys +1 hex of Energy Draining Field radius per level, up to 3 levels, applying to every field on the hull. The cost is the rules' own formula - 50 points per hex added, that is 300 × (radius + 1) for the first level - summed over each field the ship mounts.
  • On a Variable field the refit raises the normal-power radius only: the double-power radius does not move, so every level bought is one level of boost made redundant. Once the two meet, boosting the field buys nothing.

Energy Draining Mine

  • A ballistic launcher that fires sensor probes at a hex, rather than at a unit.
  • The launcher stores 3 probes and begins the battle with 1, gaining one per turn - a critical result slows that to one every two turns, then every three, and so on. Any number of stored probes may be launched, at any number of hexes, in one turn.
  • Scatter follows the rules' table: 75% the probe lands on the chosen hex, 15% it scatters up to 5 hexes, 10% it fails to arrive.
  • A probe that lands deals no damage, but projects an Energy Draining Field over its hex and the six around it for one turn, with every field rule above - the targeting penalty and its own fleet's immunity included. It drains on the turn it lands and on the turn after, so a unit caught by both escalates.
  • The orb shows on the map as a purple seven-hex disc. It cannot be targeted: its structure is indestructible, and destroying it would achieve nothing anyway. A blast that happens to cover its hex still resolves normally against everything else standing there.

Energy Draining Net

  • Links up with other Nets in the same fleet, turning empty space into an Energy Draining Field.
  • Any two active Nets of the same fleet within 3 hexes of one another link, and the hexes between them join the Energy Draining Field - two hexes at range 3, one at range 2, none at range 1 (adjacent Nets still count as linked).
  • Where linked Nets form a closed ring, the area enclosed is filled with field as well. A chain of Nets is not a ring and fills nothing; a ring with a trailing Net keeps its ring. The fill is capped by the number of Nets involved, and an over-large enclosure fills nothing at all.
  • Where several corridors of equal length are possible, the game picks one for you, preferring the corridor that catches the most enemy units.

Electronic Warfare Detector

  • Reads the configuration of enemy EW suites and reports it to the whole fleet, giving every friendly unit within 20 hexes the Expert Scanner benefit: a point of EW may be held back and spent late, once movement has been resolved.
  • In Fiery Void that late window is usually at the start of the Firing phase - or the Pre-Firing phase if there is one - and it uses the same EW buttons as the Initial Orders menu.
  • Multiple Detectors are cumulative, but their efficiency degrades: detectors 1-4 allow 1 saved point each, 5-8 half a point each, and 9 or more a quarter each. Fractions of a quarter and a half round down; three quarters rounds up.
  • The saved point comes out of unspent DEW only. A ship that has already committed all its EW to OEW, CCEW or other EW functions has nothing left to save, and the figure in the ship window falls as you allocate during Initial Orders.
  • Range is measured from the unit's post-movement hex, so a ship that drifts out of range of every Detector finds its saved EW allowance is zero (and it is simply treated as DEW, as usual). ELINT ships may save points too, on the same terms.

Sensor Charge Transceiver

  • Only found on the Scribe, and unlike other weapons it does not shoot at anything. Instead, the player plots a course for a sensor charge, which damages every enemy unit in the hexes it passes through (Standard 6d10, no overkill, three turns to recharge).
  • Plotting: click hexes to lay down waypoints. A leg must run along one of the six hex axes, and the first leg is the launch, which must leave along an axis the mount's own arc covers. After that the charge steers freely.
  • The charge has 16 hexes of range and 4 manoeuvres. Changing axis at a waypoint costs 1 manoeuvre for 60 degrees, 2 for 120 and 3 for a reversal; the launch itself is free. Boosting the transceiver in Initial Orders buys extra hexes or manoeuvres from one shared pool - which a level goes to is worked out from the course you actually plot.
  • While the weapon is selected, the map shows the course with direction chevrons, the hexes reachable from the head of the course in arc blue, and a spent-of-total readout for hexes and manoeuvres (plus the shared boost pool, when the weapon is boosted). Green hexes mark where something happened - where the charge manoeuvred, the head of the course, and any hex where you named a unit to hit.
  • Where several units share a hex, which to hit is your choice, through the Target Ship button on that hex's tooltip.
  • The course ends when it reaches a friendly transceiver - the charge is home, and that transceiver recharges faster for having caught it. You can always re-select the weapon and fly on past a receiver if you would rather.

Gravitic Drives and Advanced Armour

  • Walker hulls are gravitic: they can manoeuvre even while pivoted or pivoting, using the thrusters that suit their current orientation, and their thrusters ignore the first 'Efficiency Reduced' critical they receive.
  • Every Walker unit carries Advanced Armour, and every Walker ship mounts Self Repair.

Walker Jump Drives

  • Every Walker ship and every Mapmaker probe carries a Walker jump drive - an Ancient jump drive (see About the Ancients), so it opens no jump points and jumps to hyperspace at the end of the turn it is set to - with two differences: a Walker unit may fire normally on the turn it jumps, and its drive has no chance of failure, however badly damaged.
  • A Mapmaker flight jumps as one: set Jump to Hyperspace on any probe's drive, and the whole flight leaves at the end of the turn.

Extra-Dimensional Jump Drive

  • The Wanderer, Traveler, Waymarker and Guideship carry an Extra-Dimensional Jump Drive, which can drag an enemy unit into hyperspace over several turns. In Initial Orders, select the jump drive and click the enemy unit. The declaration is visible to everyone from the Movement phase, as a purple "Abduction" marker on the target. No line of sight is needed.
  • Terrain can be abducted too - asteroids, moons, fixed jump gates and shipyards, whoever bought them, and including the large multi-hex terrain no weapon can shoot at. Jump points cannot be abducted, and neither can fighter flights. A terrain unit has no EW of its own, so one point of OEW on it satisfies the EW condition below - but a unit occupying more than one hex needs every hex it stands in inside your connected Energy Draining Field, not just its centre.
  • The first turn only takes hold of the target: it costs no extra power and delivers no power-turns. If the conditions below are met, the abduction has begun, and from the next turn the drive keeps targeting it automatically, and power can be applied in the drive's menu.
  • Each power level costs the drive's power requirement again and delivers 1 power-turn: normal power is 1, double power is 2, up to 4. The power comes off the reactor like a boost, so Walker ships must shut systems down to pay for it.
  • The abduction only takes hold if, at the end of that first turn's Firing phase, both of these hold for the ship carrying the drive:
    • the target ended its movement in an Energy Draining Field connected to that ship's own field - extended through Energy Draining Mines, Nets or other Walker ships, but not through an enemy's field;
    • that ship's OEW on the target is higher than the target's DEW, counting defensive ELINT support (supporting and blanket DEW) but not offensive ELINT support.
  • Once the abduction has begun, the field and EW conditions are not checked again. Power-turns must be consecutive: the abduction continues while at least one Extra-Dimensional Jump Drive that took hold of the target keeps applying power every turn. If none does, it ends, and everything built up so far is lost.
  • The cost is the target's ramming factor / 50 power-turns, or / 10 if it has advanced armour, rounded up, with the ramming factors of any units attached to it added. What it carries in its hangars or docking bays does not count. The cost is shown in the jump drive's menu, is fixed on the turn the abduction takes hold, and does not fall as the target is damaged.
  • When the total is reached, the target is removed to hyperspace, as if it had jumped out itself, taking anything attached or docked with it. Progress is shown on the target's tooltip and ship window ("Being abducted"), and in the combat log every turn - including the reason a turn did not count.
  • Deactivating the jump drive cancels its abduction, and a drive destroyed during an attempt delivers nothing that turn - the combat log says the abduction was cancelled.
  • The Pathfinder and Scribe cannot begin an abduction, but once a friendly Extra-Dimensional Jump Drive has taken hold of a target they may target it too, adding half a power-turn each turn by applying double power to their drive. Mapmaker probes cannot take part.
  • After taking part in an abduction, however it ends, a jump drive recharges for its own jump delay before it can begin another.
  • A drive cannot abduct and jump to hyperspace on the same turn - setting Jump to Hyperspace withdraws the abduction.
  • Unlike the Walkers' ordinary jump, an Extra-Dimensional Jump Drive that is damaged may be destroyed: on every turn it is abducting, it rolls for detonation at half the percentage of its boxes lost, as other Ancient jump drives do. Its own jump to hyperspace keeps no chance of failure.
  • Not implemented: abducting friendly units. Asteroids and moons use the same ramming-factor cost as anything else, rather than the rulebook's "10 × radius cubed" - so a moon is a very long job indeed.

Hangar Requirements

  • Walker fighters are Mapmaker Sensor Probes, and they occupy their own 'Mapmaker Probes' capacity category. They may be bought with no hangar space at all - a Walker fleet needs no carrier to field them.
  • Under the standard Fleet Composition rules, the 50% full-hangar rule still applies to the capacity a Walker hull declares. Walker ships are not exempt: a hull with Mapmaker Probe capacity must have at least half of it filled with probes, exactly like any other hangar in the game. A Traveler with no probes in the fleet fails the Fleet Checker.
  • Mapmaker Probe capacity per hull - both the control / hangar figure and the basis of the half-full requirement:
    • Traveler - 36 probes (at least 18 required)
    • Waymarker - 18 probes (at least 9 required)
    • Pathfinder - 6 probes (at least 3 required)
    • Guideship - 6 probes (at least 3 required)
  • Ships count towards a Traveler's capacity too. 24 of the Traveler's 36 boxes are its Docking Bay (see below), so every Waymarker (24 boxes), Pathfinder or Guideship (12) and Scribe (4) in the fleet counts its boxes towards the Mapmaker Probe requirement - up to 24 boxes for each Traveler in the fleet, because a ship only counts for a bay it could actually sit in. A Pathfinder or Waymarker cannot meet its own probe capacity that way. Example: a Traveler and three Scribes (12 boxes) need only 6 more probes to reach the 18 required.

The Traveler's Docking Bay

  • The Traveler's aft hangar is a Docking Bay: 24 boxes that take Mapmaker Probes exactly like an ordinary hangar, and also whole ships - Scribes (4 boxes each), Pathfinders and Guideships (12 each) and a Waymarker (24). Probes and ships share the one pool, so the bay holds 24 probes, or 6 Scribes, or 2 Pathfinders, or one Waymarker, or any mix that adds up to 24.
  • Only one type of craft may launch or be recovered through it per turn - on a turn the Scribes use it, the probes cannot, and the other way round. Its launch rate is 12 Mapmakers, or 2 Scribes, or 1 Pathfinder, Guideship or Waymarker per turn, launches and recoveries together. Mapmakers auto-fill the Traveler's two side hangars before the Docking Bay, which is the only one that can hold a ship.
  • To dock, a ship must end its movement in the Traveler's hex, on the Traveler's heading, with at least 1 thrust unspent, while the Traveler is at speed 0 - the same conditions as an LCV docking on a rail. A docked ship is off the board. It launches at the Traveler's position, heading and speed, takes the usual launch initiative penalty that turn, and cannot launch on the turn it docked.
  • Ships may also start the battle aboard: during Deployment, select the ship and click the Traveler to dock it straight into the bay. A Traveler arriving as a reinforcement can bring Scribes, Pathfinders, Guideships and a Waymarker in its Docking Bay as well as its probes, and they arrive docked.
  • Any unit ordered into or out of any hangar during the Firing phase now says so on itself: "Docking with <ship>" or "Launching from <ship>", on the map tooltip and as a cyan banner on its ship window. (A flight being launched from scratch has no unit to label until the order resolves, so it carries no banner.)
  • A stowed unit's fleet-list row - a docked ship, a docked fighter flight or a rail-parked LCV alike - scrolls the map to the carrier holding it on left-click, since that is where it actually is. Right-click, or the ⓘ button, opens its own ship window.
  • A docked ship's weapons keep recharging while it is aboard, exactly as on the board; an Energy Draining Mine launcher restocks up to its usual 3.
  • Docked craft keep projecting what they project. An Energy Draining Field or Net on a ship inside the bay stays operational, and a docked EW Detector still lets the fleet save EW points within its 20 hexes - both measured from the Traveler's hex, so the bubble travels with the carrier. A docked Guideship's radius-4 field is drawn on the Traveler's own icon.
  • The Traveler's Self Repair also repairs the ships it carries. Damaged Structure, C&C and Self Repair on a docked ship, and any repairable critical on any of its systems, appear in the Traveler's own repair list, marked with the owning ship's name. They are ordered by priority alongside the Traveler's own entries, and nothing is pinned below anything else, so a docked hull may be repaired first if you want it repaired first. A docked ship's own Self Repair keeps working as well, and spends its own points before the Traveler spends any - so a docked Scribe can have its Thruster mended out of its own pool while the Traveler mends its Structure.
  • Docked ships share power with the Traveler. Every 4 points of spare reactor power across all the ships aboard give the Traveler 1 extra point: the surpluses are added together first, and the total is then divided by four and rounded down, so 4 shared gives 1, 7 gives 1 and 8 gives 2. Docked probes share nothing. The donor is not charged for it - what it costs is having the spare power in the first place, which is why you can now manage a docked ship's power from its own ship window during Initial Orders: open it from its fleet-list row (right-click, or the ⓘ button) and switch systems off, boost, overcharge and so on, exactly as you would on the board. Power a docked Scribe's thrusters down and the Traveler's available power goes up on the same click. The Traveler's Reactor tooltip shows how much is being shared and how much surplus is being pooled to get it, and the opposing player sees the same figure on the Traveler's reactor once Initial Orders are committed - sharing power is public, even though what is in the bay otherwise is not.
  • Damage to the bay never forces a ship out - probes are evicted first as boxes are lost. If the Docking Bay or the Traveler is destroyed, every docked ship is forced out and takes the bay's damage plus 2d10 to its Structure.
  • A Waymarker takes two turns to dock, and two to launch. At 24 boxes it fills the whole bay, and it does not go straight in. Order the dock as usual and, when the turn resolves, the Waymarker clamps to the Traveler's aft instead: it is still on the map, in the Traveler's hex, and stays there for the whole of the following turn before going inside at the end of it. Launching runs the same two steps backwards - it comes out onto the aft, rides there for a turn, and is a free ship again the turn after. Its 24 boxes are held from the moment the order resolves, so nothing else can be loaded into the bay while it is on its way in or out.
  • While it is riding the Traveler's aft, a Waymarker:
    • moves with the Traveler and cannot steer itself - it has no movement of its own to plot;
    • may not fire, and may not intercept - its weapon menu and firing-mode selector are withdrawn for the turn;
    • may still be shot at normally, and hits on the Traveler's own aft section are rolled on the Waymarker instead - on whichever of its Front or Aft sections has the most Structure remaining. The Traveler is hit on the ordinary hit chart first; only a hit that lands aft transfers;
    • uses no EW - it makes no allocations of any kind - and no ship may target it with EW, friendly or hostile. It keeps its own defensive EW, so it is no easier to hit than usual.
    The map tooltip and its ship window both show "Docking with <ship>" (or "Launching from") for the whole of that turn.
  • If the Docking Bay or the Traveler is destroyed while a Waymarker is riding it, the Waymarker simply lets go - it was clamped to the outside of the hull rather than stowed inside, so it takes none of the fragment damage a docked ship takes.

Mapmaker Sensor Probes

  • The Walkers' only small craft, and the only fighter flight in the game with an EW allowance of its own: 3 points per flight per turn, split freely between OEW and DEW exactly as a ship spends its scanner output, with anything unspent becoming DEW when Initial Orders are committed. It is 3 per flight, not 3 per craft. Their own mine-detection allowance is a separate pool, bought as usual with the flight's Offensive Bonus.
  • They need no hangar space at all in the Fleet Checker, so a Walker fleet may take them whether or not it brings a carrier - but any Walker hull that does declare Mapmaker Probe capacity must still have at least half of it filled. See Hangar Requirements above.
  • Every probe also carries a Medium Lightning Array - the only weapon in the game that several craft fire as one gun. A lone array cannot fire at all: three or six probes must declare together, at the same target and in the same firing mode, and the group resolves as a single shot.
    • 3-Probes - 4d10+12, fire control +2/+4/+6, -1 to hit per 3 hexes.
    • 6-Probes - 8d10+12, fire control +5/+5/+4, -1 to hit per 4 hexes. Note that it is better against fighters and worse against capital ships than the 3-probe group: the two modes are a real choice, not an upgrade.
  • A flight of six may therefore fire two 3-probe groups (at the same target or at two different ones) or one 6-probe group. Four or five declaring in 3-probe mode fire one group and waste the rest, and the game warns you before the click lands.
  • A probe that has taken ANY damage cannot contribute to a group - not merely a destroyed one. A battered flight loses the weapon before it loses the craft.
  • The array recharges over 4 turns, but it does begin the battle fully charged, so a flight may fire a combined shot on turn 1. Unlike the ship-mounted arrays it is not an accelerator: waiting longer buys nothing.
  • The flight cannot fire its Medium Lightning Arrays and its Light Chromatic Pulsars in the same turn - the restriction is flight-wide, not per craft, so one probe firing its pulsar locks the arrays out for every other probe as well.
  • The array locks on with the flight's own EW, exactly as a ship would: it does not use the Offensive Bonus; it uses its own fire control, the flight's OEW on the target is added to the roll, and the target's defensive EW (DEW, plus any blanket or supported DEW) counts against it just as it would against a ship's shot. With no OEW allocated to the target it has no lock-on, and its range penalty is doubled as usual.
  • The Light Chromatic Pulsar keeps the flight's Offensive Bonus, and adds the flight's OEW on the target less the target's defensive EW, never below +0 - so enemy DEW can cancel the OEW but never eats into the bonus, and the pulsar never suffers the no-lock penalty. Against a target with 5 DEW, 3 OEW adds nothing; against one with 2 DEW, it adds +1.
  • The array deals Flash damage, so it scores no collateral damage at all against a target standing inside any Energy Draining Field - the Walkers' own included.
  • Every probe carries a Walker jump drive - see Walker Jump Drives above. The flight opens no jump points; it jumps to hyperspace as one, at the end of any turn Jump to Hyperspace is set on one probe's drive, and may fire that turn.

Star Trek units, designed by Wolfgang Lackner-Warton and Marcin Sawicki from an original conversion by Tyrel Lohr.

Because of the vast differences in timeline (and technology) between the series, every faction is - or at least may be - split into three sub-factions, representing distinct, arbitrary eras: early (the Enterprise series), TOS (the original series) and TNG (The Next Generation, Voyager, Deep Space Nine...).

Star Trek units share a set of concepts and technologies that make them behave differently from their B5 counterparts:

Impulse Drives

  • Star Trek units generate and use thrust in their own way. They do have an engine (which can be damaged or destroyed as usual, and allows overthrusting), but its base output is very low - even 0 in some cases. Each Warp Nacelle also generates thrust, and the total is shown to the player as the Engine's output. Using that thrust is free (no thrusters are needed); Fiery Void ships do have thrusters (with unlimited capacity, and impossible to damage), but for technical reasons only.
  • As well as generating thrust, Warp Nacelles count as jump drives, so essentially every Star Trek ship - and some small craft as well - can travel faster than light on its own. Nacelle criticals cannot reduce their output below 0, but Engine criticals can; and if the Engine is destroyed, the whole manoeuvring system stops working, even if the Nacelles are still operational.

Star Trek Shields

  • Star Trek units use energy fields (the Shield Projection system) that absorb incoming damage: they reduce it a little "for free" (call it armour), then some more at the cost of their own health.
  • Absorption applies to every impact separately (every rake - and in general every non-overkill damage allocation - counts as a separate impact). A shield projection brought to 0 health stops working (it no longer weakens incoming shots in any way), but it is not destroyed, and can work again if replenished. Only destroying the associated structure block destroys a shield projection.
  • Shields are replenished at the end of the turn (after firing) by surviving Shield Projectors. Each Projector replenishes only its own projection, whatever its arc (the arc is used for things like called shots). Projectors are NOT needed for the Projection itself to work: they can be switched off (and their power used elsewhere), and the Projection stays at its current strength - it just will not regenerate after damage.
  • The oldest ships use Polarized Hull Plating instead of Shield Projection: technically it behaves exactly the same, but it has no Projectors, so it cannot be regenerated during the battle.
  • Fighter shields have no separate Projectors either - they regenerate on their own (at double the rate if the fighter is not using its direct-fire weapons).
  • Although they are called shields (and are, in the lore), technically they are a different kind of system from B5 shields: shield-related special properties (such as the Phasing Pulse Cannon family ignoring shields) do not apply when hitting a Star Trek unit.
  • Because of the shield mechanics (and the change of tactics they call for), Star Trek ships may perform slightly above their price in very small battles and duels.

Independent Fighters

Most Star Trek fighters and shuttles need hangar slots to deploy, just like their B5 counterparts. But some have a Warp Drive of their own and high endurance, and need no hangars despite being fighter-sized. They are subject to the "number of ships on one hull" limit (each flight counting as one ship), and are usually marked as Restricted Deployment.

Self-Reliant LCVs

Most Star Trek LCV-sized ships are fully capable of operating on their own: they need no hangars, and have regular sensors (rather than the restricted B5 'LCV Sensors').

Militarised Shuttles

Most Star Trek factions have no dedicated fighters, but their shuttles are usually armed and combat-capable when needed. Ships of these factions list their shuttle capacity (usually omitted in Fiery Void), and have combat shuttle designs available. Combat shuttles are not fighters, so they need not be taken if the player does not want them.

Star Trek: Federation

The United Federation of Planets is a conglomerate of separate species, each independent enough to run its own warships, built to different specifications - which the fleet list marks. This is NOT a fleet composition restriction, though: a Federation fleet may use any mix of units (although small craft should be carried by carriers of the same species).

Star Wars units, designed by Wolfgang Lackner-Warton and Marcin Sawicki.

Given the nature of the Star Wars universe, essentially any faction can use almost any unit; the main users are noted, though.

  • Fighters: for fleet design, fighters are assumed to need hangars (even hyperdrive-equipped ones), and any fighter can use any hangar, taking more or less space. So small craft are grouped into "Fighter squadrons" and "Assault squadrons" and limited accordingly (a superheavy fighter squadron and a light fighter squadron need the same hangar space, but the latter has far more craft). The Fleet Checker handles Star Wars small craft properly.
  • Weapons are portrayed as long-ranged but wildly inaccurate - hence Pulse mode with a very low base number of hits. This keeps the very high weapon counts of Star Wars units manageable: they are grouped into batteries that fire as single weapons in Fiery Void.
  • Going by their look and effects in the films, Star Wars laser and turbolaser weapons count as Particle class, with everything that implies.
  • Durability: Star Wars units are quite durable for their size, and shields add to that. The shields are hard to take out, too (their projectors have a high hit-point count and a low place on the hit table), and have a number of exceptions, based on how we see them working: notably, they are more effective against Raking weapons, while SW Ion, Ballistic and Matter weapons simply bypass them.
  • Sensors are generally lower-tech than B5 ones, which shows in their low output and limited boostability (ELINT boostability is not reduced, though). LCVs do have full sensors, without the B5 LCV Sensor limits.

The Polaren use this to aid their light combat vessels with an LCV Controller that raises the initiative of all friendly LCVs - an increase that can be boosted with extra power, representing the Polaren integrating more information and data processing into the polair.

An insectoid race that began as a typical queen-and-worker society, but won its freedom during the Enlightenment of Fire and is now fiercely individualistic.

The Coalition had no traditional military when it first reached for the stars: its early units are all civilian designs, using industrial systems as makeshift weapons.

As the Sal-bez grew (and discovered several technology caches left by an earlier power), they began to field purpose-built combat vessels, which would eventually make the Coalition one of the most powerful Nexus factions. If you are used to the traditional B5 powers, the Sal-bez are the most similar, without many unusual systems or rules.

Swarm Torpedo

One of the most iconic systems of the later Sal-bez ships. It fires multiple small ballistics at a target - hence its common name - giving long-range firepower, and stripping systems off a target so that follow-up laser hits do more damage.

Potentially the most aggressive faction in the Nexus setting, the Velrax had fought the Brixadii Clans, Dalithorn Commonwealth and Makar Federation by the early 2100s. They are the most prolific users of fighters, backed by a rigid fleet structure.

The Velrax field well-armed "strike" carriers, supported by anti-ship "gunships", and everything revolves around supporting their fighter operations. The carriers use plasma waves to break up enemy forces at range, and the gunships use plasma and lasers to pick off anti-fighter units. Some Velrax ships also carry plasma arcs, to strip armour off their targets and leave them more vulnerable to laser and fighter fire. Their heavy fighter mounts a potent anti-ship gun, later retrofitted to some of the interceptor fighters when the Velrax realised their opponents did not use fighters much.

Plasma Arc

A small, raking plasma weapon designed to strip armour from the target.

Dart / Streak Interceptors

Small torpedoes that can use OEW, used to engage enemy fighters.
