Many factions use missiles. When you buy a missile-carrying ship in Fleet Selection, you can also buy any missile ammunition its faction has available.

Ships generally come preloaded with basic ammunition for their launchers, and any missiles you buy are in addition to these.

Variety, not quantity

The number of any one missile type you can buy is limited by magazine size, but the total number of missiles is not. So your magazine may show more missiles than it can actually hold - but you cannot launch more than the magazine's total. In short, extra missiles buy you variety, not extra missiles in the magazine!

Any missile in the magazine can be fired by any launcher able to fire it: missiles are not tied to particular mounts. Weapons that store their ammunition on the mount itself are different, but they can usually only hold one kind. That matters most for fighters, since a ship's cavernous magazine is unlikely to run out during a battle.

Fighters usually start with an empty magazine (although a missile entry is present, for technical reasons), and lose a missile they are carrying if they take 2 or more damage from a single shot. They can dock on friendly carriers to reload the missiles they started the game with, as long as the carrier has bought the Ordnance Reserve enhancement (see Ship Enhancements) and there are enough ordnance points to pay for the replacements.

Missiles do not use the firing ship's OEW: their built-in guidance is usually folded into the weapon's fire control rather than kept separate. Fighter missiles do benefit from the fighter's offensive bonus.

Ammunition listings usually give the year each missile type became available (its ISD); where there are two dates, the first is for the Kor-Lyan only. Fiery Void does not enforce these, so players need to.

The tables below give each missile type's base stats. The launcher can modify them - an L-Rack Missile Launcher, for example, adds +10 to the listed ranges.

Unit enhancements are generally quite pricey, though not always. In pickup battles they are very much optional - if the game description does not say, check with your opponent that they are OK to use.

Their main uses are as an option in a scenario, as a campaign tool (representing units gaining experience, say), or as a way of spending the last few points of your fleet.

Some represent regular unit options rather than true enhancements - fighter Navigators, for example, or Markab Religious Fervor. And not every enhancement improves a unit: some, like Poor Crew, make it worse, and reduce its price to compensate.

  • Ship Enhancements
  • Fighter Enhancements
  • Mine Enhancements
  • Faction Enhancements - options for one faction only.
  • System Enhancements - refits bought for a single system rather than the whole unit.
  • Officers - specialists posted in one of a ship's systems, or in a fighter flight's first fighter.

Everything else here is bought for a whole unit. System enhancements (or "refits") are different: each one is bought for a single system on a single ship - so you can put Gunsights on one Twin Array and leave the other three alone, or harden the armour on just the reactor.

They are bought from the ship window in Fleet Selection, rather than from the ship's buy window, and like the other enhancements they are entirely optional - worth agreeing with your opponent before using them in a pickup battle.

Refits represent field modifications made by a younger race with the tools it actually has, so they are only available to Young and Middleborn ships. Ancient and Primordial vessels cannot buy them, and neither can an Ancient-technology weapon bolted onto a younger hull. The one exception is the Walkers' Wide-Beam Lightning Array refit, which is for Ancient hulls only (see Factions & Tiers). Refits are also for ships only - fighter flights, mines and terrain cannot be refitted.

Buying and using them

  1. Buy the ship in Fleet Selection as normal, then open its ship window from your fleet (your own bought ships, not the Store list).
  2. Click the system you want to refit. The same pop-up menu used for pre-battle damage appears, with its Damage and Critical Effects sections.
  3. The gold Enhancements section at the top of that menu lists every refit the system can take, each with [-] [ n ] [+] controls. The price on a row is the cost of the next level, so it rises as you buy; the running total spent on that system shows underneath as "Refits: n pts", and your fleet points update as you click.
  • A system with no gold section at all simply has nothing to offer - the normal case for most systems on most ships.
  • A refitted system gets a gold ✦ star on its icon (a Structure block, which has no icon, on its health bar after the section name), and the ship's Enhancements box gains a "System Enhancements (n)" line, where n is the number of systems refitted (a gun carrying two different refits still counts once). Both markers are shown to your own side only.
  • What a system is actually carrying is shown in its own information tooltip, alongside its normal stats.
  • Refits are a Fleet Selection purchase only. They cannot be bought, changed or removed once the battle has begun, and anything clicked after you press Ready is not submitted.

  • Refits are charged against your fleet budget like anything else. If a refit would take you over, the purchase is refused and put back as it was.
  • Pre-battle damage and refits share the same menu, so it is quite possible to wreck something you have just paid to improve. Damage wins: destroying a system removes its refits and refunds the points immediately, with a message naming what went. Destroying a Structure block takes every system in that location with it. This is one-way - unticking Destroy does not bring the refit back; you simply buy it again.
  • Editing a ship keeps its refits; changing the ship to a different class clears them and refunds the points. Copying a ship copies its refits as well.
  • Refits are saved with a fleet list. When the fleet is loaded again, every refit is re-checked against the ship as it is designed now and re-priced, since ships are revised from time to time. If a refit has become more expensive, cheaper, or is no longer possible at all, you are told what changed rather than it happening silently.
  • Finally, be aware that the ✦ star and the "System Enhancements (n)" line are hidden from your opponent, but several of the improved values themselves are not: shield ratings, armour and thrust all have to reach the other player for their own damage and movement displays to be correct. A careful opponent comparing a ship against its published control sheet can still work out that something has been refitted.

Expert officers are specialists a ship or a fighter flight can carry. Each one works from a particular system - their post - and gives their bonus for as long as they are able to serve.

Like enhancements, officers are optional: worth agreeing with your opponent before using them in a pickup battle.

Buying officers

  • Officers are bought with the unit in Fleet Selection, in the Officers section at the bottom of its buy window.
  • A few officers serve in a system you choose - the Expert Gunner in a weapon, the Matter Weapons Expert in a Matter weapon instead of the C&C, the Expert Religious Leader in any Primary system that is not a weapon instead of the C&C, the Expert Targeter in a Structure block, and the Expert Turret Officer in a turret-mounted weapon. Buy one there once the ship is in your fleet: click the system in the ship window (for a Structure block, its health bar) and add him under Officers in its Enhancements menu. A system holds one such officer.
  • Changed your mind? In Fleet Selection any officer who can move can be moved, for free - see Moving an officer.
  • A ship may carry several officers, but only one of each type - except where a card gives a higher limit. A fighter flight may carry only one officer in total: once one is bought, the buy window refuses a second.
  • A ship officer's price is a percentage of the ship's base cost, rounded up. A fighter officer's is a percentage of one fighter's cost - paid once for the whole flight, or for every fighter in it, as each card says.
  • A unit is only offered an officer whose post it has - no C&C, no Helmsman. Ancient units, OSATs, MicroSATs, mines, terrain and remotely controlled craft such as Orieni Hunter-Killers (they have no pilots) cannot carry officers at all.
  • Some officers belong to particular factions, or are not available to some, and some factions pay less for one. Each card says so.

Losing an officer

  • Disabled. If the officer's post is destroyed - by damage to the system itself, or because the Structure block holding it is destroyed - the officer is out for the rest of the battle. Repairing the system afterwards does not bring them back.
  • Killed. Whenever the post takes damage, its normal critical roll also decides the officer's fate: a roll of 20 or more, modifiers included, kills them. The roll is made even when the damage destroys the system.
  • Either way, the officer still serves for the rest of the turn it happens, and is gone from the next turn.
  • An officer whose post is destroyed by pre-battle damage is removed and refunded at once, and a message says so. Repairing the system afterwards does not bring him back - buy him again if you want him. (A Matter Weapons Expert whose weapon is only damaged before the battle stays aboard but will not serve: move him to an undamaged weapon.)
  • Fighter officers fly in the flight's first fighter and are lost only with it: when it is destroyed or drops out. There is no kill roll, and docking never loses them.
  • If the flight splits in two - part of it launched or docked on its own, or escaping a lost hangar as a separate "Split" flight - the officer goes with his fighter: the part that has it gets his bonus, and carries his cost in the fleet list, from the next turn; the rest of the flight does not.

Moving an officer

  • Some officers can change posts: the Expert Gunner, between weapons; the Matter Weapons Expert, between the C&C and the ship's Matter weapons; the Expert Religious Leader, between the systems of the ship's Primary section that are not weapons; the Expert Turret Officer, between turret-mounted weapons; and the Expert Targeter, between the ship's outer Structure blocks - or, on a medium ship, from one side to the other with the Front / Aft buttons that take Move's place. Click a block's health bar to reach his menu, and a highlighted health bar to move him there.
  • In Fleet Selection a move is free and takes effect at once. Click the system the officer is posted in: its menu's Officers section has a Move button beside his points. Press it, then click one of the highlighted systems in the ship window; a click anywhere else calls it off. An officer bought at the C&C in the buy window is shown in the C&C's menu, so he can be moved from there.
  • In Initial Orders, click the system the officer serves in. Its menu shows an Officers panel: press Move Officer, then click one of the highlighted systems in the ship window. Cancel Move keeps him where he is, up until you commit your orders.
  • He serves nowhere for the rest of that turn - none of his bonuses apply - and takes up his new post at the start of the next turn.
  • He can move to any system he could have been bought for (the Matter Weapons Expert also to the C&C, the Religious Leader to any Primary system that is not a weapon), but never to a destroyed system, or to one another officer who can move holds or is moving to that turn. (Officers who never move, such as a Helmsman at the C&C or a Technician in the Reactor, simply share their post with him.) Officers cannot swap posts in a single turn. A disabled or killed officer cannot move, and an officer can move once a turn.
  • On the turn he moves he still counts as being at his old post: damage there can disable or kill him. If his new post is destroyed that turn, he arrives to a wreck and is disabled.
  • Once-per-battle abilities belong to the officer, not the system: one he has used stays used after he moves.

Seeing your officers

  • A gold double-chevron insignia marks the post's icon in the ship window - for a fighter officer, the flight's first fighter, and for an Expert Targeter his block's health bar, after its name. It turns grey once the officer is out of action, with a red slash through it if they were killed. On the turn an officer moves, the insignia on both his old and his new post turns blue.
  • The post's information tooltip names the officer and what they do, and says how and when they were lost - or, for an Expert Scavenger, when he spent his once-per-battle save, and for an Expert Gunner, which of his once-per-battle abilities he has used. While an officer is moving, both posts' tooltips say where he is going or coming from. A fighter officer is named in the flight's own tooltip, shown when you hover over a fighter, and an Expert Targeter in a tooltip of his own when you hover over his block's health bar.
  • The ship window's gold Enhancements box counts the officers aboard, as Officers (2). In the lobby, a fighter flight's box names its one officer instead (Expert Pilot (3) for three pilots).
  • Your opponent sees none of these until the game is over. Some effects do show, though, because they change numbers everyone can read: a Helmsman's, a Security Officer's, a Religious Leader's or a Dogfighter's initiative (and the initiative, profiles and sensors of a ship whose Religious Leader has been lost), a Geneticist's profiles, a Technician's reactor power, an ELINT Officer's sensor rating, ELINT ranges and Blanket Protection, a Matter Weapons Expert's, an Expert Gunner's or an Expert Turret Officer's fire control, a Plasma Scientist's battery capacity and Plasma Web range, a Navigator's turn, pivot, roll and accel/decel costs, a Jump Officer's jump range, how long his jump point stays open and his arrival initiative penalty, an Evader's or Expert Pilots' offensive bonus (and the jinking the pilots let a flight do), the criticals and damage an Engineer or Scavenger repairs, and a weapon's loading when an officer speeds up its reload. The opponent sees the number, not who is behind it. An Expert Gunner's added Piercing mode stays hidden from your opponent until he fires it: they see it only in the replay of that turn. Lasers boosted by an Expert Laser Technician, and ion guns boosted with thrust by an Expert Ion Technician, do not show as boosted to your opponent, though the extra damage shows once the shots land.

5% of the ship's base cost.
Limit
1 per turret - a ship may carry one on each of its turrets. Vree Conglomerate only.
Notes
He is disabled if his own weapon is destroyed, even while the rest of his turret fights on, and his kill roll is that weapon's critical roll (his -1 included). Officers for fighter flights. A flight may carry only one of them, and he flies in its first fighter: he is lost if that fighter is destroyed or drops out, and goes with it if it is split off from the flight (see Losing an officer). The one exception is the Expert Pilot: a flight may carry up to four of them (in place of any other officer), one in each of its first fighters.

Each officer's cost is for a standard flight of six fighters. A flight of another size pays it in proportion, rounded up: three fighters pay half, nine one and a half times, twelve double. A single superheavy fighter pays it in full. The Breaching Expert is the exception: he serves in one pod, so his cost is the same whatever the size of its flight.
