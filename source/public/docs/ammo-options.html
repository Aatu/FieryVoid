Many factions use missiles. When you buy a missile-carrying ship in Fleet Selection, you can also buy any missile ammunition its faction has available.

Ships generally come preloaded with basic ammunition for their launchers, and any missiles you buy are in addition to these.

Variety, not quantity

The number of any one missile type you can buy is limited by magazine size, but the total number of missiles is not. So your magazine may show more missiles than it can actually hold - but you cannot launch more than the magazine's total. In short, extra missiles buy you variety, not extra missiles in the magazine!

Any missile in the magazine can be fired by any launcher able to fire it: missiles are not tied to particular mounts. Weapons that store their ammunition on the mount itself are different, but they can usually only hold one kind. That matters most for fighters, since a ship's cavernous magazine is unlikely to run out during a battle.

Fighters usually start with an empty magazine (although a missile entry is present, for technical reasons), and lose a missile they are carrying if they take 2 or more damage from a single shot. They can dock on friendly carriers to reload the missiles they started the game with, as long as the carrier has bought the Ordnance Reserve enhancement (see Ship Enhancements) and there are enough ordnance points to pay for the replacements.

Missiles do not use the firing ship's OEW: their built-in guidance is usually folded into the weapon's fire control rather than kept separate. Fighter missiles do benefit from the fighter's offensive bonus.

Ammunition listings usually give the year each missile type became available (its ISD); where there are two dates, the first is for the Kor-Lyan only. Fiery Void does not enforce these, so players need to.

The tables below give each missile type's base stats. The launcher can modify them - an L-Rack Missile Launcher, for example, adds +10 to the listed ranges.

Unit enhancements are generally quite pricey, though not always. In pickup battles they are very much optional - if the game description does not say, check with your opponent that they are OK to use.

Their main uses are as an option in a scenario, as a campaign tool (representing units gaining experience, say), or as a way of spending the last few points of your fleet.

Some represent regular unit options rather than true enhancements - fighter Navigators, for example, or Markab Religious Fervor. And not every enhancement improves a unit: some, like Poor Crew, make it worse, and reduce its price to compensate.

Everything else here is bought for a whole unit. System enhancements (or "refits") are different: each one is bought for a single system on a single ship - so you can put Gunsights on one Twin Array and leave the other three alone, or harden the armour on just the reactor.

They are bought from the ship window in Fleet Selection, rather than from the ship's buy window, and like the other enhancements they are entirely optional - worth agreeing with your opponent before using them in a pickup battle.

Refits represent field modifications made by a younger race with the tools it actually has, so they are only available to Young and Middleborn ships. Ancient and Primordial vessels cannot buy them, and neither can an Ancient-technology weapon bolted onto a younger hull. The one exception is the Walkers' Wide-Beam Lightning Array refit, which is for Ancient hulls only (see Factions & Tiers). Refits are also for ships only - fighter flights, mines and terrain cannot be refitted.

Buying and using them

  1. Buy the ship in Fleet Selection as normal, then open its ship window from your fleet (your own bought ships, not the Store list).
  2. Click the system you want to refit. The same pop-up menu used for pre-battle damage appears, with its Damage and Critical Effects sections.
  3. The gold Enhancements section at the top of that menu lists every refit the system can take, each with [-] [ n ] [+] controls. The price on a row is the cost of the next level, so it rises as you buy; the running total spent on that system shows underneath as "Refits: n pts", and your fleet points update as you click.
  • A system with no gold section at all simply has nothing to offer - the normal case for most systems on most ships.
  • A refitted system gets a gold ✦ star on its icon, and the ship's Enhancements box gains a "System Enhancements (n)" line, where n is the number of systems refitted (a gun carrying two different refits still counts once). Both markers are shown to your own side only.
  • What a system is actually carrying is shown in its own information tooltip, alongside its normal stats.
  • Refits are a Fleet Selection purchase only. They cannot be bought, changed or removed once the battle has begun, and anything clicked after you press Ready is not submitted.

  • Refits are charged against your fleet budget like anything else. If a refit would take you over, the purchase is refused and put back as it was.
  • Pre-battle damage and refits share the same menu, so it is quite possible to wreck something you have just paid to improve. Damage wins: destroying a system removes its refits and refunds the points immediately, with a message naming what went. Destroying a Structure block takes every system in that location with it. This is one-way - unticking Destroy does not bring the refit back; you simply buy it again.
  • Editing a ship keeps its refits; changing the ship to a different class clears them and refunds the points. Copying a ship copies its refits as well.
  • Refits are saved with a fleet list. When the fleet is loaded again, every refit is re-checked against the ship as it is designed now and re-priced, since ships are revised from time to time. If a refit has become more expensive, cheaper, or is no longer possible at all, you are told what changed rather than it happening silently.
  • Finally, be aware that the ✦ star and the "System Enhancements (n)" line are hidden from your opponent, but several of the improved values themselves are not: shield ratings, armour and thrust all have to reach the other player for their own damage and movement displays to be correct. A careful opponent comparing a ship against its published control sheet can still work out that something has been refitted.
