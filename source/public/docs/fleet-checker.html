The Fleet Checker tells you whether your fleet meets the standard fleet composition rules, and highlights anything that needs a manual check or your opponent's agreement.

It is advice, not a gate. If you would rather not follow these rules, or need something different for a scenario, the game will not stop you - as long as your fleet stays within its points allowance. Just make sure you and your opponent are on the same page, and have fun!

For a standard, reasonably balanced match, though, it is a good idea to stick to the Fleet Checker's rules.

AoG created a set of tournament rules so that players could compete on fair terms. Fleets built to them tend to be well-rounded and make for fair battles, so they are worth using outside tournaments too.

Assumptions

  • A 3500-point game.
  • A fixed 42×30 map.
  • Each player uses only one faction.
  • No Ancient or ISA fleets.

The original rules

  • At least one Jump Drive.
  • At least one Capital Ship.
  • Every unit of LCV size or smaller must have hangar space in the fleet - you cannot bring fighters based elsewhere.
  • At least half of the fighter hangars must be filled, so you cannot bring (too many) empty carriers. This only applies to actual fighters, not to other craft such as assault shuttles or LCVs.
  • Deployment restrictions observed (see Deployment Restrictions).
  • Variant restrictions observed (see Variant Restrictions).
  • At most two Uncommon units and one Rare unit in the fleet, on top of the variant restrictions.
  • At most three ships based on the same hull (fighters and other small craft are exempt).
  • No static objects (bases, OSATs, mines...).
  • Only genuine combat units - no transports (not even military ones) and no diplomatic ships.
  • Some factions were deemed not tournament-legal - notably the ISA (an all-White Star fleet) and the Ancients.

Fiery Void modifies the rules above so that they balance different fleet sizes and Ancient fleets. This tailoring is the FV team's own and is not official B5 Wars in any way, although it was inspired by the tournament rules.

For a balanced game under these rules, it is best to use:

  • A fixed 42×30 map (or similar).
  • One faction per player.
  • Fleets of a similar power level on both sides - Factions & Tiers groups the available fleets into subjective power levels suitable for balanced pickup battles.

What the Fleet Checker checks

  • At least one Jump Drive.
  • A minimum number of Capital Ships (you can take more) - see the table below.
  • Most units of LCV size or smaller need hangar space in the fleet. There are exceptions, especially for custom factions from other universes.
  • At least half of the fighter hangars must be filled. This only applies to regular fighters - not to assault shuttles, LCVs or even SHFs. A few craft that can be bought without any hangar space still fill their own hangar category, and that category must be half full in the same way - Walker Mapmaker Sensor Probes are the example.
  • At most one flight smaller than 6 craft (only checked for craft whose maximum flight size is 6 or more).
  • Variant restrictions observed (see Variant Restrictions).
  • Deployment restrictions observed (see Deployment Restrictions).
  • Uncommon and Rare units are limited across the whole fleet, on top of the variant restrictions - see the table below.
  • A maximum number of ships that are variants of the same hull (units that need hangar space are exempt) - see the table below.
  • No static objects (bases, OSATs).
  • No non-combat units (freighters, exploration ships, VIP transports...).
  • Non-standard elements (enhancements, custom units, Special variants) are pointed out to you, but they do not mark the fleet as incorrect.

Every unit in the game has a variant rating, in one of these categories:

Common (C)
A common example of what is built on a given hull. No limit.
Uncommon (U)
Not as common - found in numbers only alongside their more mundane cousins. For every three units of a given hull, or fraction thereof, one may be Uncommon.
Rare (R)
Very rare units, almost never deployed in numbers (often command variants). One in every three Uncommon slots may be taken by a Rare unit instead.
Unique (Q)
Only very few exist - possibly just one. Treat them as Rare for fleet selection, with the added limit that no more than one may be present.
Special (X)
Non-standard restrictions that need the player's attention; the actual restriction is in the unit's description. The Fleet Checker will NOT check these correctly, but points them out with a note.

Fighters

The rule is exactly the same for fighters, but you count whole flights instead of individual craft.

Sportsmanship

Please do not abuse variable flight sizes - keep your restricted flights the same size as the regular ones! The checker will let you take two full-sized Rare flights alongside eight single-craft Common "flights", but it really is not good sportsmanship.

Every unit in the game also has an availability rating:

Unlimited (100%)
The unit is common in its fleet.
Limited (50%)
Common enough, but still should not make up the bulk of a fleet (mostly used by Ancient fleets).
Limited (33%)
Mostly specialist support units, such as dedicated missile ships and ELINT ships.
Restricted (10%)
The rarest units, such as the newest or experimental designs and the largest battleships.
Unique (Q)
Only very few exist - possibly just one. Treat them as Rare for fleet selection, with the added limit that no more than one may be present.
Special (X)
Non-standard restrictions that need the player's attention; the actual restriction is in the unit's description. The Fleet Checker will NOT check these correctly, but points them out with a note.

Put simply, the total value of the units in a category cannot exceed its percentage of your fleet allowance - for example, you can spend up to 33% of it on Limited (33%) units. Two further rules apply:

  • If you have only one unit in a category, it may exceed the limit (mainly so that such units can be taken in small battles).
  • A Restricted unit needs another unit as an escort - such rare vessels are not usually allowed to wander about on their own.
