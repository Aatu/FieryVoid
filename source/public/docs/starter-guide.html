New to the game? The FAQ has tips on getting a first game going, and the Fiery Void Discord is the place to ask for help.

  1. On the main page, look under Join Games for a game to join, and click it.
  2. Check the game's Scenario Description: its creator may have set restrictions, such as "tournament rules" or "no Minbari".

No games listed under Join Games? Create one of your own (see Creating a Game).

  1. Click Create Game on the main page.
  2. Give the game a name that says what it is - for example Centauri (me) vs Narn (you) or All Welcome 5000 pts.
  3. Choose a different background picture if you like.
  4. The default settings - two teams of one slot, 3500 points each - are fine for your first game, so go straight on to Confirm & Create Game. You are taken to Fleet Selection, and as the creator you are put in the first slot automatically.

More advanced settings

  • A larger map: choose a bigger Map Template, such as Base Assault, or No Boundaries. It gives more room to manoeuvre, but gives ships and races with long-range weapons a relative advantage.
  • Add Slot adds another player slot to a team. Use it to add more players to the game, or to give yourself a second slot for part of your fleet that arrives later in the game (a Delayed Deployment Slot).

Buying ships

  1. In the Purchase Fleet panel, click Choose Faction and pick a faction to list its ships. Each ship's point cost is shown beside it, with its variants listed underneath in a darker font. Show details opens a ship's control sheet (see Ship Tooltip & SCS).
  2. Click Add to fleet. A window opens where you can rename the ship and choose any options or enhancements available to it (usually for a points cost). Confirm the purchase to add it to your fleet.
  3. The ship appears in Your Fleet and your points total goes up. Every bought ship has Details, Edit, Copy and Remove links.

Fighter flights are bought on their own, and you can set how many fighters are in a flight. Full flights are usually better, unless you are squeezing the last few points out of your fleet.

Saved fleets

To get started quickly, load a ready-made fleet: open Load a Fleet, choose one of the default fleets, then click Ready.

As you get to know Fiery Void you can add your own: build a fleet and click Save Fleet, and from then on it is listed under Load a Fleet too.

Fleet building conventions

A few conventions are not enforced, but should generally be followed in pick-up games unless you agree otherwise. They are often called "tournament rules", and the Check button shows how your fleet measures up at any time (the full rules are in Fleet Checker). The main points:

  • Each side should have at least one capital ship.
  • Each side should have at least one ship with a jump engine - a blue hexagonal icon on a ship's SCS. Most capital ships have one, and sometimes a Heavy Combat Vessel or even a Medium Ship does. One is enough: it explains how the fleet got to the fight!
  • Fighters can only be taken if the fleet has ships with the hangar space to carry all of them into battle.
  • Any ship that can carry fighters should bring at least half its maximum load. An Earth Alliance Omega Destroyer carries 24 fighters, for example, so it must bring at least 12. Two ships that each carry one flight (two Hyperions, say) can share a single full flight to meet the half-hangar minimum for both.
  • Special fighters (Rutarians, Thunderbolts and so on) need a ship that can carry that kind of fighter - a Dargan or Balvarix for Rutarians, an Omega or Warlock for Thunderbolts.
  • Only one flight per side may have fewer than 6 fighters.
Tip

You are unlikely to hit the points limit exactly, but you should get within 100 points of it. Enhancements on specific ships are a good way to use up the last few points and make your fleet stronger.

Ready

When you are happy with your fleet, click Ready and confirm. The game begins once every slot is ready. If it does not load by itself, go back to the main page and click the game under Your Games.

Once a game has started, these are the two main places to find out about your ships.

Ship tooltip

Point at a ship, or left-click to select it, to show its tooltip:

Ship name
The name its player gave it when building the fleet.
Defence rating
Front-aft / sides: the % chance for a shot to hit the ship before any other adjustments. Defensive EW lowers the "current" figure below the "base" one by 5% per point.
Turn cost / delay
The turn cost times the ship's current speed is the thrust needed to turn one hex side; the turn delay is how many hexes it must move before it can turn again.
Initiative
The ship's place in this turn's initiative order. Initiative is rolled on 1d100 plus any adjustments (for ship type and so on); lower initiative moves earlier.
Speed
How many hexes the ship moves in the Movement phase unless it accelerates or decelerates. In brackets is its acceleration rating - the thrust cost to change speed by 1. Any turn delay carried over from the last turn is listed too.

Ship Control Sheet (SCS)

Right-click a ship to open its SCS: a window showing all of its systems as icons, with the front of the ship at the top. Weapons are red; other systems are generally blue, with some exceptions.

  • Health: a dark green bar along the bottom of each icon shows the system's remaining structure. It turns orange when the system is critically damaged, and is empty (with the whole icon dimmed) once it is destroyed. A lightning bolt over an icon means the system is powered down.
  • Charging: a weapon shows its charge as turns charged / turns needed, and is ready to fire when the two numbers match. A weapon that is not ready has a haze over its icon.
  • Details: point at a system to see all of its information, including the effects of any critical damage.
  • Structure: each section's structure is shown in its header bar as current / maximum, then its armour - 540/600 A8, for example.

You need the SCS to change power settings in Initial Orders, and to fire weapons in the Fire Orders phase.

Right-click a fighter flight for the same view of the flight: each fighter's remaining / total structure, and whether it has been destroyed or has disengaged. Point at a fighter's weapons to see their details.

Moving around the map

Zoom in and out with the mouse wheel. Hold down the right mouse button to drag the map.

  1. A new game starts in the Deployment Phase. Left-click one of your ships to select it (a circle marks it), then left-click a hex inside your deployment zone to place it there.
  2. Set its starting speed (0 to 10) with the + and - beside the forward green arrow, which starts at 5. You can also start facing a direction other than towards the enemy, using the curved green arrows.
  3. When all your ships are placed, click the green Ready tick at the top of the screen. The title bar changes from Deployment to Waiting for turn.

Once every player has deployed, the first turn begins: the bar on the left shows the ships' initiative order, and the title bar reads Initial Orders.

  • Fighter flights are deployed on the map, or docked inside a carrier's hangar - see Hangar Operations.
  • A slot set to deploy later in the game takes no part in the Turn 1 deployment. It gets a Deployment Phase of its own when its turn comes (see Delayed Deployment Slot).

Every game turn runs through the same phases, in this order:

  • Pre-Turn Ordersonly when needed
  • Initial Orderspower, EW, ballistics
  • Movementship by ship
  • Pre-Firing Ordersonly when needed
  • Fire Ordersassign weapons
Pre-Turn Orders
Decisions some factions make at the very start of the turn. Only happens when there is such a decision to make.
Initial Orders
Adjust power, assign EW and fire ballistic weapons.
Movement
Move your ships: accelerate or decelerate, move, slide, turn, pivot and roll.
Pre-Firing Orders
Some special weapons fire before all the others; when they do, this phase happens.
Fire Orders
Assign weapons to fire.

All the players complete each phase at the same time - except Movement, which is done one ship at a time (or in groups, when Simultaneous Movement is in use).

When you have finished a phase for all your ships - or finished moving one ship or flight - click the green tick in the title bar at the top of the screen. A box pops up to confirm or cancel: click its green tick to confirm.

Some factions have special rules or abilities that need a decision at the very start of the turn, before Initial Orders. If one of your ships has a system or ability like this, you are given the chance to make that decision in the Pre-Turn Orders phase.

The phase only happens when there is a decision to make; otherwise the turn starts straight away with Initial Orders.

In Initial Orders you set each ship up for the turn:

  • Electronic Warfare - assign the ship's sensor rating to EW functions.
  • Ballistic weapons - missiles, torpedoes and Energy Mines must be fired in Initial Orders.
  • Power - turn systems off for extra power, and use spare power to boost systems such as the sensors or engines.

By default each ship keeps last turn's power settings, and all of its EW starts on Defensive EW.

When all your ships are done, click the green tick in the title bar and confirm; the title changes to Waiting for turn. Once every player has committed, the Movement phase begins with the first ship in the initiative order.

Electronic Warfare (EW)

Left-click one of your ships to select it. Its EW assignments are shown in its tooltip, and in a box to the right of its SCS.

Defensive EW (DEW)
Makes your ship harder to hit, by everything except fighters.
Close Combat EW (CCEW)
A bonus to hit any fighters that come within 10 hexes.
Offensive EW (OEW)
Select an enemy ship to see its OEW buttons. OEW gives you a bonus to hit that ship, and just 1 point gives you a lock-on. Without a lock-on an enemy ship is very hard to hit: weapon range penalties against it are doubled.

Share the ship's EW points between DEW, CCEW and each of its OEW lock-ons with the + and - buttons in the ship tooltips.

EW tips
  • It is wise to lock on to no more than 1-3 enemy ships at a time.
  • Some captains alternate between "turtling" (all EW on defence) and a "hard lock" (all EW as OEW on a single target), especially at long range.
  • Others prefer a balanced approach: half on DEW, and 1-2 points of OEW on 2-3 targets.
  • A few points of CCEW are a good idea when enemy fighters are around, even just as a deterrent.

Ballistic weapons

  1. Right-click one of your ships or flights to open its SCS, and select one or more ballistic weapons.
  2. Point at a target ship. If it is in arc and range, each weapon's % chance to hit is shown - it may still change after EW assignments and defensive fire.
  3. Left-click to fire. A red targeting reticle appears beside the target; your opponent sees it once Initial Orders are over.

For hex-targeted weapons (such as Narn Energy Mines), select the weapon and click the target hex. Only your team sees the hex; the enemy just sees that something was fired.

The Ballistic Lines buttons on the right-hand side of the screen switch ballistic lines on and off. Enemy ballistic attacks become visible once both players have committed their Initial Orders.

Power management

Managing power is optional, and new players should probably skip it at first: it is certainly useful when done well, but it can backfire when done badly. If you want to try it:

  1. Right-click a ship to open its SCS.
  2. Left-click systems to turn them off with their red power button. A lightning bolt marks a powered-down system, and the Reactor's value goes up to match.
  3. Use the + button on boostable systems, such as the Scanner or Engine, to give them extra power. Their number goes up and the Reactor's reserve goes down.
Negative power

If a ship's power rating is negative - from a Reactor critical hit, for example, or a ship built with a power deficit such as the White Star - you cannot commit your Initial Orders until you have turned off enough systems to bring the Reactor to 0 or more.

Fighter flights cannot adjust power or EW, so they have nothing to do in this phase unless they carry ballistic weapons.

Power tips
  • Prefer turning off weapons with a recharge rate of 1, for flexibility.
  • If you need to, turn off rear-facing weapons before forward ones.
  • By convention, Jump Engines are only powered down once they are more than 50% damaged, except in desperate scenarios.

Ships move one at a time, in initiative order. When it is your ship's turn, green arrows appear around it for each move it can make - point at one to see what it does.

The ship's tooltip shows its current movement figures too: turn cost, turn delay, pivot cost, roll cost, acceleration cost, thrust remaining and so on.

  • A ship must always move exactly its Speed in hexes - no more, no less. The large green arrow at its front disappears once it has.
  • Anything that needs thrust (everything except moving straight forward) opens a window at the bottom of the screen, where the thrust is assigned to your thrusters. Usually the game assigns it for you and you just click the green arrow in the box - or the red cancel circle to think again.
  • Sometimes you must assign the thrust yourself, usually because there is a choice of thrusters (pivoting, rolling), because some thrusters are damaged or destroyed, or because you are overthrusting - putting more thrust through a thruster than its rating, which may critically damage it at the end of the turn.
  • Click the red Cancel Move icon behind your ship to take back your moves one at a time; right-click it to undo the whole move.

When the move is complete, a green tick appears in the title bar. Click it and confirm: the title shows Waiting for turn, and the next ship in the initiative order takes its move.

Adjusting speed

  • Speed can only be changed before any other manoeuvre. Use the + and - near the straight green arrow at the front of your ship, and watch the turn cost and turn delay change with it.
  • You cannot decelerate if your bow thrusters are destroyed, or accelerate if your aft thrusters are destroyed. Pivoting can help, but it is not an easy manoeuvre to get right.
  • Once you make any manoeuvre (moving forward included) the + and - disappear, and you cannot change speed again this turn - unless you take back all your moves with the Cancel Move icon and start again.
  • After adjusting speed you may move, slide, turn, pivot and/or roll.

Moving forward

  • Click the large green arrow, usually at the front of your ship, to move forward one hex. Right-click it to move forward as many hexes as you have left.

Sliding

  • Left-click one of the shorter green arrows to slide: the ship moves one hex diagonally forward without changing its facing, for a thrust cost of 1/5 of its speed.
  • You cannot slide if the opposite side thruster is destroyed, and you must make a normal forward move before sliding again.

Turning

  • Click one of the single curved arrows to turn. You may assign extra thrust to shorten the turn delay this turn leaves you with; extra thrust goes to the opposite side thruster first, then the aft thrusters.
  • The turn arrow disappears until you may turn again - that is, until you have moved forward as many hexes as your current turn delay. Turn delay can carry over into the next game turn.
  • You cannot turn if the opposite side thruster is destroyed. If all your aft thrusters are destroyed, you can only turn when your speed is low enough for the turn cost to be 1 (paid with the opposite side thruster).
  • When moving backwards, use your bow thrusters in place of the aft thrusters for turning.

Pivoting

  • A pivot changes a ship's facing without changing its direction of movement. Click the two overlapping curved green arrows in the direction you want to pivot, then assign thrust by hand to thrusters on two adjacent sides - once you pick one thruster, the game tells you which other to use.
  • This starts a pivot of one hex side per turn, which carries on until you click the overlapping curved blue arrows to stop it. A ship cannot pivot faster than that unless it is Agile, but it may start and stop a pivot in the same turn.
  • While pivoting - and while pivoted 60 or 120 degrees, even with the pivot stopped - a ship cannot accelerate or turn, and cannot decelerate unless it has a Gravitic engine (the Minbari, for example).
  • Once the ship has pivoted 180 degrees and stopped, or pivoted back to 0 degrees and stopped, it may accelerate, decelerate and turn again.
  • Pivoting is usually used after the bow thrusters have been destroyed, to turn 180 degrees and decelerate with the aft thrusters. It takes three turns to pivot all the way round, and you can only start decelerating on the fourth, so it needs very careful planning - or desperation!
  • Fighters can save unused thrust for combat pivots in the Fire Orders phase, to change direction after the Movement phase - but each pivot then costs 2 thrust instead of 1.

Rolling

  • Click the circular arrow behind the ship to start a roll, and the blue icon to stop it. A roll takes a whole turn unless the ship is Agile, so its sides do not swap until the next turn.
  • While rolling, a ship swaps its port and starboard facings every turn. It cannot normally turn, pivot or slide, but it can change speed.
  • A ship can make an Emergency Roll while pivoting if it has to, but it is risky: it forces a critical roll on the ship's C&C.

Jinking (fighters only)

  • A fighter flight has a jagged "lightning bolt" trailing its icon, with + and - buttons. Use + to assign thrust to jinking.
  • Each level of jinking lowers the chance to hit the flight by 5% - but lowers the flight's own chance to hit by the same amount.
  • Fighters in the same hex as enemy fighters are dogfighting: they ignore the defensive benefit of their target flight's jinking (but not if they are jinking themselves).

A few special systems - such as the Torvalus Transverse Drive - fire before all other weapons. If one of your ships has such a system and it is ready to fire, you are given the chance to use it in Pre-Firing Orders.

They are fired exactly as described under Firing Orders, but their effects are fully resolved before anyone gets to fire anything else.

Firing takes three steps: select your weapons, check their chance to hit, and target.

Selecting weapons

  • Right-click one of your ships or flights to open its SCS. Pointing at a weapon shows its firing arc around the ship, including its maximum effective range - handy for deciding which weapons to fire at which targets.
  • Click each weapon you want to fire, once; it turns blue. Or right-click a weapon to select every active weapon of the same name.

Chance to hit

  • Point at a target ship. Each selected weapon's % chance to hit is listed below the ship's information (you may need to drag the map up to see it, depending on where the target is).
  • This is the actual chance to hit, allowing for the target's defensive EW, your offensive EW, the weapon's fire control against that type of target, and the range penalty. Hover over it in the targeting tooltip for the full breakdown. The only thing it leaves out is the enemy ship's defensive fire.
  • A negative chance to hit is shown as "- XX%". A weapon that does not have the target in arc, has no line of sight or is out of range shows a message instead (such as "Not in arc"), and cannot fire at that target.

Targeting

  • Happy with the odds? Left-click the target reticle in the enemy ship's tooltip. Your selected weapons turn orange to show they are assigned to fire.
  • From then on the chance to hit is no longer shown (except for Ballistic or Split Targeted weapons).
  • Repeat these steps for each weapon you want to fire this turn, at the same enemy or at different ones.

Firing modes

  • Some weapons have alternative firing modes - a Battle Laser can fire in Raking or Piercing mode, for example. Left-click such a weapon and letters appear at the top right of its icon.
  • Click the right-most letter to cycle through the modes.

Defensive fire

  • Weapons with an intercept rating and a 1-turn recharge that have not been assigned to fire will automatically fire defensively at incoming enemy fire within their arcs. To see what was applied, hover over a "chance to hit" with a dotted underline in the Combat Log.
  • Lasers and some other weapons cannot be intercepted. Particle weapons (pulse weapons included) and plasma weapons can, as can most others. The FAQ has the full rules.

Combat pivots

  • A fighter flight with thrust left over may make a Combat Pivot in the Fire Orders phase to bring its weapons to bear - often on fighters that moved later in the initiative order.
  • Click the crossed curved green arrows in the direction you want to pivot; a pivot in combat usually costs 2 thrust instead of 1. Once you are facing the right way, assign fire orders as normal.

Finishing the turn

  • If you do not want to fire at all (everything is out of range or arc, say), just click the green tick at the top of the screen - the game will not move on until you do.
  • Once all your ships and flights have their fire orders, click the green tick in the title bar and confirm; the title shows Waiting for turn. As in the other phases, the game moves on once every player has committed - this time to the next turn.
  • To see what happened, click Replay: Fiery Void takes you back to the previous turn and shows the Firing phase as an animation. For a quicker look, the COMBAT LOG tab at the bottom of the screen lets you read the firing log of earlier turns.
